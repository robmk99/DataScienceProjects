# Two-Leg Delta-Neutral Execution in Crypto: Basis (Spot vs Perp) and Cross-Venue Perp-vs-Perp

Scope: how spot/perp cash-and-carry and cross-venue perp/perp positions are entered and exited at size — leg sequencing, legging risk, spread-based execution logic, hedge latency, funding dynamics, venue constraints, and documented practitioner evidence. CEX vs DEX (Hyperliquid) distinctions are flagged explicitly. Information current to Sept 2026.

---

## KQ1. Leg-sequencing approaches: passive-then-hedge vs simultaneous IOC vs quoting the spread

### Takeaway
The industry-standard vocabulary (per Talos, the main institutional multi-leg algo vendor) is three modes: (1) **Opportunistic Passive** — rest a maker order on the *initiating* (less liquid) leg and hedge aggressively on the liquid leg after fill, with a passive timeout and max-imbalance guard; (2) **Aggressive** — cross both legs at once, tolerating slippage to minimize leg risk; (3) **TWAP-style paced spread execution** that only crosses when behind schedule or when leg imbalance grows. Open-source bots (Hummingbot) implement the two extremes: spot-perp arb uses simultaneous taker orders on both legs; cross-exchange market making (XEMM) uses maker-on-illiquid / immediate taker hedge on liquid. Atomic spread matching (Deribit combo/spread orders, Paradigm block RFQ, OneChronos in equities) eliminates legging risk entirely but is only available where the venue supports it.

### Cited Findings
- Talos' multi-leg algo framework describes three execution strategies: **TWAP** ("Passive, schedule-based execution; crosses only when behind schedule or imbalance grows; best for steady spreads and deep liquidity"), **Aggressive** ("Tolerates slippage to minimize leg risk; prioritizes flat positioning over price improvement"), and **Opportunistic Passive** ("Maker on initiating leg with configurable passive timeout; falls back to aggressive hedging when conditions breach thresholds"). — [Talos, Mastering Multi-Leg Algos](https://www.talos.com/insights/mastering-multi-leg-algos-advanced-execution-strategies-in-crypto-markets)
- Talos' key parameters for a multi-leg algo: **Initiating leg** ("typically the less liquid instrument"), **Limit price** ("target spread guiding execution bounds"), **Max Imbalance** ("maximum allowed notional difference between legs"), **Passive Timeout** ("duration before switching to aggressive hedging"), **Hedge Pay Extra** ("additional slippage tolerated to flatten risk"), **Market Hedge Trigger** ("P&L or volatility-based hedging activation"). Use cases listed include cross-venue arbitrage, cash-and-carry, delta-neutral staking, and client facilitation hedging with derivatives. Talos states "No single 'best' configuration exists — setup depends on liquidity depth, spread volatility, time/imbalance appetite, and venue capabilities." — [Talos](https://www.talos.com/insights/mastering-multi-leg-algos-advanced-execution-strategies-in-crypto-markets)
- Hummingbot **spot-perpetual arbitrage** (v1 strategy): both legs use **taker orders** (`market.get_taker_order_type()`); prices are obtained via `get_order_price()` for the configured `order_amount` (i.e. size-aware VWAP, not mid) with four concurrent price requests (spot buy/sell, perp buy/sell); both orders are submitted back-to-back in `execute_arb_proposal()`; a per-leg **slippage buffer** (default 0.05 = 5%) widens the limit price so the taker order fills; the code has "no explicit retry logic, timeout handling, or sequential dependency" and "lacks explicit handling for scenarios where one leg fills while the other fails." — [Hummingbot source](https://github.com/hummingbot/hummingbot/blob/master/hummingbot/strategy/spot_perpetual_arbitrage/spot_perpetual_arbitrage.py); parameters `min_opening_arbitrage_pct` (default 1%), `min_closing_arbitrage_pct` (default 0.1%), `next_arbitrage_cycle_delay` (120 s), `perpetual_leverage` (1x) — [Hummingbot docs](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/spot-perpetual-arbitrage/)
- Hummingbot **cross-exchange market making (XEMM)** is the canonical passive-then-hedge implementation: it "emits limit orders to a less liquid, larger spread market; and emits market orders on a more liquid, smaller spread market whenever the limit orders were hit." It "would always immediately hedge any order fills from the maker side, regardless of how profitable the hedge is at the moment." Parameters: `min_profitability`, `slippage_buffer` (default 5%, added to taker price), `top_depth_tolerance`, `order_size_taker_balance_factor` (99.5%), `order_size_taker_volume_factor` (25% — caps hedge size relative to taker-side tradable volume), `limit_order_min_expiration` (130 s), `active_order_canceling`. — [Hummingbot XEMM docs](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/cross-exchange-market-making/)
- A known Hummingbot defect illustrates a common engine bug: the spot-perp strategy "does not check that the order size exceeds the minimum tradable in each leg before executing any of them," so one leg can be rejected after the other fills. — [Hummingbot issue #5299](https://github.com/hummingbot/hummingbot/issues/5299)
- **Atomic spread execution (CEX derivatives):** A Deribit futures spread order "is a single order that will result in the execution of trades in two futures simultaneously, where one future will be bought and the other will be sold." On Paradigm/Deribit block trades the minimum is $200,000 per block, "and when executing a two-legged spread, this can be as low as $100,000 per leg"; Paradigm does a price-band check at quote time but Deribit re-checks at execution, and "this timing difference between the two checks can potentially lead to trade rejections." — [Paradigm/Deribit spread blocks](https://www.paradigm.co/blog/executing-futures-spreads-as-block-trades-with-paradigm-and-deribit); [Deribit Futures Spread Order](https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/25944776836893-Futures-Spread-Order)
- **Atomic pairs (equities analogue):** OneChronos found that across 8 equity pairs "synchrony rates never exceed 10%" (legs filling within 1 ms), i.e. >90% of paired notional is legged for some duration; their Atomic Pairs auction fills both legs simultaneously "within their respective quotes," and they report >$40MM cumulative price improvement as of May 2026. — [OneChronos Part 1](https://www.onechronos.com/insights/pairs-part-one/); [Part 2](https://www.onechronos.com/insights/pairs-part-two/)
- Practitioner descriptions of generic arb-bot design: when "a cycle fills leg 1 and then fails on leg 2, the account holds an asset it never wanted with full market exposure"; the mitigation is an `auto_unwind` that "sells the position straight back ... with a bounded LIMIT IOC." Another practitioner: "Deciding what to do in the 30 milliseconds after one leg fills and the other slips is the actual product of a production-grade arbitrage bot." — [OpsToInnovator PR #11](https://github.com/OpsToInnovator/agency-agents/pull/11); [botversusbot](https://botversusbot.com/articles/multi-exchange-arbitrage-crypto-bots-2026/)
- Liquibit (crypto market-neutral fund) executes each pair trade "almost simultaneously ... within milliseconds," trading up to 5 instruments per coin (spot, up to 3 perps, a dated future) across ~10 active exchanges, building positions "from multiple smaller trades of a few hundred dollars." — [Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/liquibit-market-neutral-crypto-strategy-traditional-trading/)

### Inferences
- Mapping to the question's taxonomy: (a) passive-on-hard-leg + aggressive hedge = Talos "Opportunistic Passive" / Hummingbot XEMM; used when the illiquid leg has wide spreads (earning the maker rebate/spread pays for the taker hedge) and the liquid leg is deep enough that hedge slippage is bounded. (b) simultaneous aggressive IOC on both = Talos "Aggressive" / Hummingbot spot-perp arb; used when the spread signal is fleeting, both books are deep, or when volatility makes unhedged time the dominant cost. (c) quoting/working the spread = Talos TWAP-with-limit-spread; used for large size where impact dominates and the basis is slow-moving (e.g., entering a multi-day carry).
- Passive-aggressive is strictly preferable when maker fees are negative/zero (see KQ5) since the passive leg earns while the hedge pays; it becomes unattractive when the liquid leg's queue is adversely selected (fills happen exactly when the market is moving against the hedge).
- Where a venue offers an atomic spread book (Deribit combos; Paradigm RFQ), the spread trade should be routed there for size; for spot-vs-perp on Binance/Bybit/OKX/Hyperliquid no atomic cross-product order exists, so the engine must manage legging itself.

### Gaps
- No public source quantifies, for crypto specifically, the fill-rate or realized-spread difference between passive-aggressive and simultaneous-IOC modes. Talos' webinar gives the framework but not numbers.
- No primary documentation from Wintermute/Jane Street/GSR on their internal leg-sequencing; only interview-level statements (see KQ7).

## KQ2. Defining and targeting the "spread"; trading the spread instead of the legs

### Takeaway
Engines compute the spread from *size-aware executable prices* (VWAP/impact prices for the intended clip on each leg) rather than mids, express it in bps of the spot price, and gate child orders on `executable_spread >= threshold` where the threshold nets fees, slippage buffer, and target carry (often converted to an annualized basis to compare against funding). Exchanges themselves define "premium index" the same way (impact bid/ask vs index), which is the natural target variable because it is what funding is computed from.

### Cited Findings
- Hummingbot spot-perp arb evaluates `profit_pct()` on proposals built from `get_order_price()` for the configured `order_amount` on each side, then opens only if `profit_pct >= min_opening_arbitrage_pct` (default 1%) and closes if `>= min_closing_arbitrage_pct` (default 0.1%); slippage buffers are applied on top of the proposal price before submission. — [Hummingbot source](https://github.com/hummingbot/hummingbot/blob/master/hummingbot/strategy/spot_perpetual_arbitrage/spot_perpetual_arbitrage.py); [docs](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/spot-perpetual-arbitrage/)
- Talos' multi-leg algo is parameterized by a **Limit price** defined as the "target spread guiding execution bounds," with pacing (TWAP) that "crosses only when behind schedule or imbalance grows." — [Talos](https://www.talos.com/insights/mastering-multi-leg-algos-advanced-execution-strategies-in-crypto-markets)
- Exchange-defined premium (the funding driver) is size-aware: OKX `Premium index = [Max(0, Impact bid – Index) – Max(0, Index – Impact ask)] / Index`, where impact bid/ask is "the average fill price to execute the impact value," with `Impact value = 200 × Max leverage` (in USD terms) for the contract. — [OKX funding fee mechanism](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/funding-fee-mechanism-eea)
- Binance premium index is "calculated every 5 seconds using impact bid/ask prices." — [Binance funding intro](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/introduction-to-binance-futures-funding-rates-360033525031)
- Hyperliquid: "The premium is sampled every 5 seconds and averaged over the hour"; HIP-3 perps use `premium = (0.5 * (impact_bid_px + impact_ask_px) / oracle_px) - 1`. — [Hyperliquid funding docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/funding)
- Binance mark price for USDⓈ-M is a median of three candidates: `Price1 = Index × (1 + LastFundingRate × TimeUntilFunding/8)`, `Price2 = Index + MovingAverage(Basis)` where the 2.5-minute basis MA = `Σ[(Bid1_i + Ask1_i)/2 − PI_i]/30` sampled every 5 s, and the last traded price. — [Binance mark price FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/360033525071)
- Ethena's "Basis Spread" doc defines a basis trade as one in which "the trader simultaneously purchases (sells) an asset and sells (buys) the futures contract," with basis converging to zero at expiry for dated futures. — [Ethena docs](https://docs.ethena.fi/solution-overview/underlying-derivatives/basis-spread)
- Deribit's example shows why the spread must be the traded variable: a March/June calendar entered at +$201.50 flipped to −$240.50 in 24 h (12–13 Mar 2020), a loss of 0.157 BTC on a $10k/$10k position despite equal notionals. — [Deribit Insights](https://insights.deribit.com/education/risks-of-futures-spread-trading/)

### Inferences
- A practical spread definition for a basis engine: `spread_bps = 1e4 × (perp_exec_price(side, qty) − spot_exec_price(side, qty)) / spot_mid`, using impact prices for the child size on each book, then `net_edge_bps = spread_bps − taker_fee_bps(leg1) − maker/taker_fee_bps(leg2) − hedge_slippage_buffer_bps`. Annualize for comparison with funding: `annualized ≈ spread_bps × (365×24 / hours_to_expected_convergence)` for dated futures; for perps, compare spread capture against expected cumulative funding over intended holding period.
- Because exchanges compute premium/funding from impact prices sampled every 5 s and averaged over the interval, a spread engine that reads the same impact-price construct is aligned with the variable that determines the funding cash flow, and can also anticipate the exchange's "predicted funding."
- Child-order pacing when spread is favorable (Talos TWAP-with-limit) is the scalable version: rest passively on the initiating leg at a price that implies the target spread given the *current* hedge-leg impact price, re-pricing as the hedge leg moves (the "chase/spread-limit" pattern).

### Gaps
- No public spec of a named exchange's "spread order" for spot-vs-perp on Binance/Bybit/OKX/Hyperliquid; spread-limit logic is implemented client-side by the trader or by a vendor (Talos).
- Typical thresholds used by professionals (e.g., "execute when basis ≥ X bps") are not published; Hummingbot defaults (1% open / 0.1% close) are retail-oriented and far wider than institutional practice.

## KQ3. Legging risk quantified and controlled

### Takeaway
The only rigorous public quantification of legging cost is from equities (OneChronos): >90% of paired notional legs, and the lagging leg slips 1.5–14 bps on average from the mid at the time of the first fill. Crypto evidence is anecdotal but consistent: hedges must land in low-single-digit ms within a region to avoid a "hedging blind spot," and during the 10/10/2025 cascade perp spreads widened 1,321× (0.02 → 26.43 bps) with depth collapsing 98%, i.e. hedge slippage can jump from sub-bp to tens of bps in a minute. Controls documented: max-imbalance/max-unhedged-notional, passive timeout, hedge-pay-extra slippage budget, volatility/P&L-triggered forced hedge, circuit breakers that pull quotes, and auto-unwind with bounded IOC.

### Cited Findings
- Equities benchmark: "synchrony rates never exceed 10% for any pair" (legs within 1 ms); lagging-leg adverse deviation from mid "ranges from 1.5 to 14 basis points depending on the pair"; asynchronous execution injects excess P&L noise "visible at every horizon and grows at longer holding periods." — [OneChronos Part 1](https://www.onechronos.com/insights/pairs-part-one/). In Part 2, "roughly half of all asynchronously executed notional" faced situations where waiting for the second leg cost more than crossing immediately. — [OneChronos Part 2](https://www.onechronos.com/insights/pairs-part-two/)
- Crypto latency: intra-region AWS Tokyo venue-to-venue latency "runs 1–2 ms, and under 1 ms over VPC Peering or PrivateLink"; cross-region "tens or hundreds of milliseconds are a hedging blind spot — during a violent move, your hedge order is still swimming through a submarine cable while the price has already left." — [longcipher, AWS Tokyo](https://book.longcipher.com/en/blog/aws-tokyo-digital-wall-street/)
- Liquibit targets execution "down to 10 milliseconds," acknowledging it "has not reached levels in equities, already below one millisecond." — [Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/liquibit-market-neutral-crypto-strategy-traditional-trading/)
- Stress-case slippage (10 Oct 2025): BTC perp spreads went "from 0.02 basis points to 26.43 basis points at peak — a 1,321x increase"; over the 40-minute cascade spreads averaged 5.92 bps vs 0.20 bps pre-cascade (30×); Binance held 2.50 bps while a smaller venue hit 13.14 bps; visible depth fell "from $103.64 million to $0.17 million — a 98% collapse"; $3.21B liquidated in the 21:15 UTC minute; 2.51-percentage-point funding divergence across exchanges. — [Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/how-3.21b-vanished-in-60-seconds-october-2025-crypto-crash-explained-through-7-charts)
- Market-maker controls in that event: LO:TECH's "risk engine's circuit breakers kicked in and pulled our quotes from the market"; "The exchange APIs were pretty unreliable at the time, so it took a bit longer to get back to full size." Wintermute (Jasper De Maere): "If we're shorting a perp for any type of hedging reasons, and then we also get liquidated on these very volatile spikes, then it becomes difficult"; Amberdata's Greg Magadini: makers "couldn't reliably hedge across multiple exchanges due to auto-deleveraging mechanisms." — [Yahoo Finance / CoinDesk](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/why-wintermute-other-market-makers-180022935.html)
- Talos control parameters: Max Imbalance (notional difference between legs), Passive Timeout, Hedge Pay Extra, Market Hedge Trigger (P&L- or volatility-based). — [Talos](https://www.talos.com/insights/mastering-multi-leg-algos-advanced-execution-strategies-in-crypto-markets)
- Hummingbot XEMM caps the hedge relative to available taker liquidity (`order_size_taker_volume_factor` 25%) and balance (99.5%) and applies a 5% slippage buffer to guarantee the hedge fills. — [Hummingbot XEMM](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/cross-exchange-market-making/)
- Auto-unwind pattern: on leg-2 failure "the executor books the loss, logs 'inventory is now unbalanced', and halts while waiting for a human"; with `auto_unwind` it "sells the position straight back to the cycle's start asset with a bounded LIMIT IOC and carries on trading." — [OpsToInnovator PR #11](https://github.com/OpsToInnovator/agency-agents/pull/11)
- Liquibit risk controls: beta-weighted dollar-neutral per exchange to reduce liquidation risk; "double the minimum margin requirement as a buffer"; leverage 3–6x; position caps 10–20% of unleveraged NAV. — [Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/liquibit-market-neutral-crypto-strategy-traditional-trading/)
- Cross-venue engine reference (open-source, paper-trading): "Contract normalisation, inverse-contract delta, supervised two-leg execution with recovery." — [delta-exchange-mt5-hedging](https://github.com/pranay123-stack/delta-exchange-mt5-hedging)

### Inferences
- Expected hedge-leg drift over Δt for a single unhedged leg can be sized as `σ_1s × sqrt(Δt)`: with BTC intraday realized vol of ~40–60% annualized (σ_1s ≈ 0.7–1.1 bps/√s), a 100 ms gap implies ~0.2–0.35 bps 1-σ drift and a 500 ms gap ~0.5–0.8 bps — small versus taker fees in calm markets, but fat-tailed: the 10/10 data show the quoted half-spread alone can reach 13–26 bps at the exact moment hedges are needed. (This computation is my inference; no source provides crypto-specific per-100-ms legging slippage.)
- The dominant control is therefore not average-case latency but *tail* handling: hard max-unhedged-notional, a hedge-pay-extra budget that widens automatically with realized vol, and a kill switch that stops initiating-leg quoting when the hedge venue's depth or API health degrades (LO:TECH's behaviour).
- Because both Binance and Bybit pin API rate limits per UID/IP (KQ6), a burst of hedge retries under stress can itself hit rate limits; engines should reserve order-rate headroom for hedges (e.g., never let quoting consume >50% of the 10-s budget).

### Gaps
- No published, crypto-specific empirical measurement of "hedge slippage vs. delay (50–500 ms)" was found; OneChronos numbers are US equities.
- No named crypto firm has published a legging-loss post-mortem; the closest are 10/10 interviews (above) and generic bot-design writeups.

## KQ4. Funding-rate dynamics and their role in timing

### Takeaway
Funding intervals are now heterogeneous and dynamic: Binance defaults to 8 h (00/08/16 UTC) but switches to hourly when the rate hits its cap and later reverts to 4 h; Bybit (since Oct 30 2025) auto-switches to 1 h at cap and may revert to 2/4/8 h without notice (BTC/ETH majors excluded); OKX supports 8/4/2/1 h with a per-interval prorated interest rate; Hyperliquid (DEX) pays hourly at 1/8 of an 8-h-equivalent rate, capped at 4%/h, computed on the oracle (spot) price. Funding is only paid to positions open at the settlement timestamp (Binance has a 15-s window), so entries/exits are typically timed just before/after settlement; premium indices are 5-s sampled and interval-averaged, which makes the "predicted" rate largely known before settlement.

### Cited Findings
- **Binance:** default settlement "every 8 hours at 00:00, 08:00, and 16:00 UTC." Since May 2 2025, "if a funding rate reaches its cap or floor at settlement, the interval automatically shifts to hourly settlements starting the next hour," continuing "until rates stabilize below 0.025% absolute value for 16 consecutive cycles, then reverts to 4-hour intervals." Formula `F = [Average Premium Index + clamp(interest − P, 0.05%, −0.05%)] / (8/N)`; interest 0.01% per 8 h; caps ±0.75 × maintenance-margin ratio for majors, ±2% for other USDT contracts. "If you close your position prior to the funding time, you will not pay or receive any funding"; a 15-second window applies to positions opened near settlement. — [Binance funding intro](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/introduction-to-binance-futures-funding-rates-360033525031)
- **Bybit:** dynamic settlement effective 30 Oct 2025 08:00 UTC (fully by 3 Nov 2025): when funding hits its preset cap/floor at settlement, "the system will automatically switch the settlement frequency to once per hour"; it "may revert to longer intervals — every two, four, or eight hours — based on market conditions and without prior notice"; the switch completes in ~4 minutes; excluded contracts: BTCUSDT, BTCUSDC, BTCUSD, ETHUSDT, ETHUSDC, ETHUSD, ETHBTCUSDT, ETHWUSDT. — [PR Newswire (Bybit)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bybit-launches-dynamic-settlement-frequency-system-for-perpetual-contracts-302598179.html); [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2025/10/29/bybit-to-roll-out-dynamic-funding-rate-settlement-feature-for-perpetual-contracts/)
- **OKX:** `Funding rate = clamp[Average premium index + clamp(Interest − Avg premium, 0.05%, −0.05%), cap, floor]`; `Interest = 0.03% / (24 / settlement interval)` (0.01% per 8-h settlement); the average premium index is a *linearly weighted* moving average over the last interval (`Σ i×P_i / Σ i`); contracts settling every 4/2/1 h get proportionally smaller per-period rates. — [OKX funding fee mechanism](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/funding-fee-mechanism-eea); [OKX formula revision](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/important-update-revision-of-the-funding-rate-formula-for-okx-perpetual)
- **Hyperliquid (DEX):** "funding rate on Hyperliquid is paid every hour"; `F = Average Premium Index + clamp(interest − P, −0.0005, 0.0005)` on an 8-h basis, paid hourly at 1/8; premium sampled every 5 s and averaged over the hour; interest 0.01%/8 h = 0.00125%/h (11.6% APR to shorts); cap 4%/hour; payment = `position_size × oracle_price × funding_rate` — "the spot oracle price is used to convert the position size to notional value, not the mark price." — [Hyperliquid funding docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/funding)
- **Mark vs last price (Binance):** mark = median(Price1 = index × (1 + lastFunding × T/8), Price2 = index + MA(2.5-min basis), last price); basis samples every 5 s. — [Binance mark price](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/360033525071)
- **Long-run funding statistics (Ethena docs):** 3-year average annualized funding ETH ~9%, BTC ~7.8%; 2024 ETH ~13% APY (OI-weighted); negative-funding days only 17.5% (ETH) / 15.9% (BTC); longest negative streak 13 days vs 176 days positive; with stETH yield (~3%) only 8.84% of days had negative total carry. — [Ethena docs (llms-full)](https://docs.ethena.fi/llms-full.txt)
- **Theory:** in a continuous-time equilibrium with risk-constrained arbitrageurs and momentum speculators, "a linear funding rule induces an endogenous mean-reverting basis." — [Zhang, SSRN 2026](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6185958). Ackerer–Hugonnier–Jermann identify funding specifications for linear and inverse contracts under which the perp price coincides with spot. — [Perpetual Futures Pricing, Math. Finance 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mafi.70018); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11771)
- **Empirical:** "The Two-Tiered Structure of Cryptocurrency Funding Rate Markets" documents via time-series econometrics and transaction-cost accounting "how real-world market frictions create exploitable yet risky arbitrage opportunities." — [MDPI Mathematics 14(2):346, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/2/346). A ScienceDirect study analyzes funding arbitrage risk/return on Binance and BitMEX vs DEXs ApolloX and Drift. — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2096720925000818)
- **Liquidation-cascade behaviour:** 10 Oct 2025 21:15 UTC, $3.21B liquidated in 60 s; $6.93B in the 20:50–21:30 window ($10.39B/h vs $0.12B/h pre-cascade); 83.9% long liquidations; funding divergence 2.51 pp across exchanges. — [Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/how-3.21b-vanished-in-60-seconds-october-2025-crypto-crash-explained-through-7-charts); total ~$19B liquidated over 10–11 Oct. — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)

### Inferences
- Because premium is averaged over the interval (uniform average on Binance/Hyperliquid; linearly weighted on OKX, so late samples dominate), an engine can compute the settlement rate with high confidence minutes ahead; the standard play is to be short-perp/long-spot across the timestamp when predicted funding is positive and above fees, and to avoid holding a paying-side position through a settlement.
- Dynamic intervals matter for execution: on Binance/Bybit/OKX a capped rate flips the contract to hourly settlement, which raises carry per day for the receiving side but also signals extreme premium — spread-based entries then face maximum adverse selection; hedging cross-venue between an 8-h contract and a 1-h contract creates a funding-timing mismatch that a perp-vs-perp engine must track per instrument (Bybit's interval can change "without prior notice").
- Hyperliquid funding on oracle price (not mark) means a perp-vs-perp hedge between a CEX (mark-price funding) and Hyperliquid has notional mismatch equal to the mark/oracle gap.
- Around liquidation cascades the basis can invert violently and cross-venue funding can diverge by percentage points — the cascade is exactly when hedges are hardest to place (KQ3), so basis entries should be paused when liquidation velocity spikes.

### Gaps
- Bybit's own help-center page (settlement times/caps) returned 503; Bybit interval details are from its press release and Invezz.
- I could not access the full text of the MDPI/ScienceDirect/arXiv papers to extract half-lives or breakeven-funding numbers; only their abstracts/summaries are cited.

## KQ5. Cross-venue specifics: tick sizes, multipliers, fees, inverse vs linear, sizing

### Takeaway
Position balancing is in *coin quantity* for linear (USDT/USDC-margined) perps (delta −1 per coin, so hedge = same coin amount as spot) and in *contract count* for inverse (coin-margined) perps where the contract is a fixed USD amount and the delta is convex in price; Ethena uses both types depending on per-venue liquidity and capital efficiency. Fees are a first-order component of the spread threshold; Liquibit's commission drag fell from 5% (2021) to 1.5% (2023). Off-exchange settlement (Copper Clearloop/Ceffu) with daily settlement is how large basis books cap exchange exposure.

### Cited Findings
- Linear contract sizing: Deribit "one BTC_USDC-PERPETUAL contract represents 0.0001 BTC"; linear perps settle and margin in a stablecoin. — [Deribit Linear Perpetual](https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/31424969384605-Linear-Perpetual)
- Inverse: "profits and losses as well as margin are paid in the base asset ... while the price of the contract is quoted in units of the quote asset"; contract size "expressed in units of the quote currency (10,000 USD)" (Kraken/Deribit-style); with an inverse perp "every change in the price of the underlying is offset by the short position ... which results in a stable equity curve when denominated in units of the quote asset." — [Bybit inverse contracts intro](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Introduction-to-Inverse-Contract); [Phemex delta hedging](https://phemex.com/blogs/what-is-delta-hedging)
- Ethena "uses both inverse (coin-margined) and linear (USDT-margined) perpetual contracts depending on liquidity and capital efficiency available per exchange," hedging on Binance, Bybit, Bitget, Deribit and OKX; backing assets stay with OES providers (Copper, Ceffu, Kraken, Anchorage) that "delegate notional value to exchanges for margin purposes" and "settle PnL on frequent cycles (daily for Copper's Clearloop)," bounding exchange exposure "to the amount accumulated since the last settlement"; roughly 50% of backing is delegated as margin; unrealized PnL is held in BTC or USDT depending on contract type. — [Ethena docs](https://docs.ethena.fi/llms-full.txt)
- Fees as spread component: Liquibit's commission costs "decreased 70% from 5% (2021) to 1.5% (2023)" of returns. — [Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/liquibit-market-neutral-crypto-strategy-traditional-trading/)
- Cross-venue price divergence in stress: Binance 2.50 bps vs Arkham 13.14 bps average spread during the 10/10 cascade; 2.51-pp funding divergence across exchanges. — [Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/how-3.21b-vanished-in-60-seconds-october-2025-crypto-crash-explained-through-7-charts)
- Cross-venue hedging engines need "contract normalisation, inverse-contract delta" as explicit modules. — [delta-exchange-mt5-hedging](https://github.com/pranay123-stack/delta-exchange-mt5-hedging)
- Binance mark price for coin-margined contracts is documented separately from USDⓈ-M. — [Binance COIN-M mark price](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/mark-price-and-price-index-of-coin-margined-contracts-fff6ec1b3f5845d19f1fb924644a0877)

### Inferences
- Sizing rule: linear perp short qty = spot qty (coins). Inverse perp: contracts = notional_USD / contract_size; coin delta of an inverse short = −contracts × contract_size / P², so the hedge must be re-balanced as price moves (a short inverse perp against long spot is *over*-hedged in coin terms as price rises and under-hedged as it falls). Ethena's note that unrealized PnL accrues in BTC/USDT reflects this.
- For perp-vs-perp across venues with different tick sizes/lot sizes, the engine should quantize both legs to the coarser lot and carry the residual as tracked unhedged delta (below a max-unhedged threshold, KQ3).
- Maker rebates/VIP tiers change the optimal sequencing: the leg with the better maker economics should be the passive initiating leg (Hummingbot XEMM logic), which is frequently the *perp* on CEXs since perp maker fees are typically lower than spot maker fees.

### Gaps
- Exact current fee schedules (VIP tiers, maker rebates) were not fetched; they change frequently and should be pulled from each venue's fee page at implementation time.
- No source quantifying typical steady-state price divergence (bps) between Binance/Bybit/OKX/Hyperliquid perps outside stress.

## KQ6. Venue order types, rate limits and latency constraints affecting engine design

### Takeaway
All four venues support post-only, IOC, FOK (Hyperliquid lacks FOK in the fetched docs) and reduce-only. Rate limits differ by an order of magnitude: Binance USDⓈ-M default 300 orders/10 s and 1,200/min per account (VIP up to 18,000/min); Bybit 20 create/s (linear), 10/s (inverse, spot) per UID with 600 HTTP req/5 s per IP; Hyperliquid (DEX) 1,200 weight/min per IP plus an address-based budget of 1 request per 1 USDC cumulatively traded (10,000 initial buffer), with batching counted as 1 IP request but n address requests. These constrain how many child orders/cancels a spread engine can issue and how much headroom must be reserved for hedges.

### Cited Findings
- **Binance Futures TIFs:** IOC, FOK, GTX (post-only; rejected orders come back as `EXPIRED` via websocket order_trade_update). — [Binance derivatives change log](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/change-log); [Binance new order](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/trade/rest-api)
- **Binance Futures rate limits:** order rate limit 1,200/min per account/sub-account; message requests 2,400/min per IP; USDⓈ-M additional 300 orders/10 s; "Any adjusted order limit will be ⅓ of the minute limit"; VIP by 30-day volume: 600M USDT → 6,000/min (72,000 msg/min), 1B → 7,200, 2.5B → 9,600, 5B → 12,000, 12.5B → 14,400, 25B → 18,000/min (300,000 msg/min); fill-ratio >1% bonuses of +15,000 to +50,000; extra allowance per bp of exchange volume share above 0.5%. — [Binance Futures rate limits](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/rate-limits-on-binance-futures-281596e222414cdd9051664ea621cdc3)
- **Bybit v5 TIFs:** GTC, IOC, FOK, PostOnly ("If the order would be filled immediately when submitted, it will be cancelled"); batch place 1–10 orders per request. — [Bybit place order](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/order/create-order); [Bybit batch](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/order/batch-place)
- **Bybit rate limits:** create order 20/s linear, 10/s inverse, 10/s spot; amend 10/s; cancel 20/s linear, 10/s others — "based on the rolling time window per second and UID"; a batch of 5 orders "will consume 5"; HTTP "600 requests within a 5-second window per IP"; websocket 500 connections per 5 min per IP; VIP/PRO tiers higher (separate page). — [Bybit rate limit docs](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/rate-limit)
- **Hyperliquid (DEX) TIFs/order types:** GTC, IOC ("unfilled part canceled instead of resting"), ALO ("add liquidity only ... canceled instead of immediately matching"), reduce-only, TP/SL, Chase (post-only that re-prices to best bid/ask, max 5 active), Scale, Trailing stop, TWAP (sub-order interval minimum 30 s, max slippage 3% per suborder, 5 min–7 days, catch-up up to 3× suborder size). — [Hyperliquid order types](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/order-types); [Hyperliquid exchange endpoint](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/exchange-endpoint)
- **Hyperliquid rate limits:** REST "aggregated weight limit of 1200 per minute" per IP; exchange action weight `1 + floor(batch_length/40)`; L2 book / clearinghouseState weight 2; websocket max 10 connections, 1,000 subscriptions, 2,000 messages/min, 100 inflight posts; **address-based**: "1 request per 1 USDC traded cumulatively since address inception" with "initial buffer of 10000 requests," and when limited "one request every 10 seconds"; cancels get `min(limit + 100000, limit × 2)`; open-order cap 1,000 (+1 per 5M USDC volume, max 5,000); "Batched requests count as one request for IP based rate limiting, but as n requests for address-based rate limiting." — [Hyperliquid rate limits](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/rate-limits-and-user-limits)
- **Latency:** co-located venues in AWS Tokyo see 1–2 ms (sub-1 ms via PrivateLink); Talos emphasizes "co-location and venue-proximate deployment." — [longcipher](https://book.longcipher.com/en/blog/aws-tokyo-digital-wall-street/); [Talos](https://www.talos.com/insights/mastering-multi-leg-algos-advanced-execution-strategies-in-crypto-markets)
- **API reliability under stress:** "The exchange APIs were pretty unreliable at the time" (10/10/2025, LO:TECH). — [Yahoo/CoinDesk](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/why-wintermute-other-market-makers-180022935.html)
- Hummingbot Bybit connector bug: spot-perp arb failed in Bybit hedge-mode ("position mode issue"), illustrating position-mode (one-way vs hedge) as an integration constraint. — [Hummingbot issue #4316](https://github.com/hummingbot/hummingbot/issues/4316)

### Inferences
- Design consequences: (1) the *passive* leg's re-quoting consumes most of the order budget (place + cancel per re-price), so Binance's 300/10 s and Bybit's 20/s bound how many symbols one account can quote simultaneously; (2) on Hyperliquid the address-based budget scales with cumulative USDC traded — a new address starts with 10,000 actions and then earns 1 per USDC, so a fresh basis engine should trade volume before it quotes aggressively, and batching helps only the IP limit; (3) hedges should use IOC (Binance IOC, Bybit IOC, Hyperliquid IOC) with a slippage-bounded limit price rather than pure market orders, and reduce-only on exit legs to avoid flipping sign if a fill notification is duplicated; (4) Hyperliquid's native TWAP (30-s min suborder spacing, 3% max slippage) is too coarse for tight basis execution — child-order pacing must be client-side.
- Talos' "Chase"-like re-pricing of the initiating leg maps to Hyperliquid's native Chase order (post-only auto-reprice), which reduces API calls on the DEX side.

### Gaps
- OKX order types/rate limits were not fetched in this pass (OKX funding was); OKX supports post_only/ioc/fok per its v5 API but I did not verify current numeric limits.
- Matching-engine internal latency figures (Binance/Bybit/OKX) are not officially published; Hyperliquid block time not in the fetched order-type page.

## KQ7. Documented practitioner evidence: how large desks run basis execution

### Takeaway
Public evidence is thin on execution internals. Ethena documents *where* and *why* it routes hedges (fees, depth, existing positions, funding/basis opportunities across Binance/Bybit/Bitget/Deribit/OKX with OES custody) and that slippage and execution fees are charged to minters/redeemers, but not order types or sequencing. Wintermute and LO:TECH describe rules-based delta-neutral frameworks with circuit breakers that pull quotes when hedging becomes unreliable (10/10/2025). Liquibit gives the most concrete public description of a systematic multi-venue market-neutral book (ms-level paired execution, 3–6x leverage, 2× margin buffer). No legging-loss post-mortem from a named firm was found.

### Cited Findings
- Ethena hedging system: "Determines where to route & execute orders upon a successful mint & redeem USDe request" considering "fees, depth of liquidity, existing positions, and other funding + basis opportunities"; ingests order-book updates, public trades and private positions/orders from CeFi exchanges, OES providers, Pyth and Redstone; calculates portfolio risk vs limits and reconciles internal vs exchange state. — [Ethena Hedging System](https://docs.ethena.fi/technical-design/hedging-system.md). "Slippage & execution fees are included in the price when minting & redeeming. Ethena earns no profit from the minting or redeeming of USDe"; hedges are "approximately the same notional size" as spot; team runs 24/7 manual oversight and can "systematically delegate additional backing assets," cycle positions between exchanges, and deploy reserves; liquidation would require a ~65% stETH/ETH discount vs historical max 8% pre-Shapella and <0.3% post-Shapella; "dynamic allocation" to stablecoins during low/negative funding. — [Ethena docs (llms-full)](https://docs.ethena.fi/llms-full.txt)
- Ethena expansion: on 25 Sept 2026 Ethena began an equity basis strategy using tokenized stocks as spot collateral and Binance equity perpetuals for hedging. — [CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/09/25/ethena-begins-equity-basis-trade-on-binance-with-tokenized-stocks/)
- Wintermute: "a very rules-based approach" requiring delta neutrality; during the cascade "the violence and the speed ... made it very difficult to continue to make markets"; LO:TECH circuit breakers pulled quotes automatically. — [Yahoo/CoinDesk](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/why-wintermute-other-market-makers-180022935.html)
- Wintermute/Abraxas built ~$600M of BTC perp shorts on Hyperliquid described as funding-capture, with Abraxas "withdrawing ETH from Binance ... consistent with accumulating spot inventory to pair against perp shorts." — [CoinOTAG](https://en.coinotag.com/abraxas-wintermute-600m-bitcoin-short-book-hyperliquid) (secondary reporting; treat as indicative, not confirmed by the firms)
- Liquibit: ~160 coins, up to 5 instruments per coin, 17 exchange connections / ~10 traded; "fully hedged at the underlying coin level"; pair trades "within milliseconds"; positions built from many sub-$1k clips; 3–6x leverage; beta-weighted dollar-neutral per exchange; 2× minimum margin buffer. — [Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/liquibit-market-neutral-crypto-strategy-traditional-trading/)
- Institutional tooling: Talos multi-leg algos are marketed precisely for cash-and-carry, cross-venue arbitrage and delta-neutral staking, with co-location. — [Talos](https://www.talos.com/insights/mastering-multi-leg-algos-advanced-execution-strategies-in-crypto-markets)
- Block/RFQ route for size: Paradigm + Deribit spread block trades, $200k block minimum / $100k per leg on spreads; Paradigm/FTX (2021) marketed spreads for "cash and carry trades and funding rate farming." — [Paradigm](https://www.paradigm.co/blog/executing-futures-spreads-as-block-trades-with-paradigm-and-deribit); [The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/ftx-and-paradigm-partner-to-launch-futures-spreads-trading/)
- DWF Labs' hedging guide (page returned 403; search-snippet only) states makers hedge spot inventory by shorting "the same amount in the futures market." — [DWF Labs](https://www.dwf-labs.com/news/understanding-market-maker-hedging)

### Inferences
- Large basis books (Ethena-scale) do not primarily rely on speed: they route by venue depth/fees/funding, use OES custody with daily settlement, keep ~50% margin delegation, and pass execution cost to the mint/redeem price. The execution engine's job is to keep realized spread within the slippage embedded in the mint quote; the "hedge latency" problem is bounded by the fact that the spot leg (mint) is received first and the perp hedge follows.
- Market makers running perp-vs-perp across venues behave as passive-aggressive engines by construction (quote on venue A, hedge on B), and their documented failure mode is not slow hedges but *inability* to hedge (ADL, API outages, liquidations on the hedge leg) — hence circuit breakers rather than faster hedging.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation from Wintermute, Jane Street, GSR, Galaxy or Two Sigma on basis execution mechanics; only interviews and secondary reporting.
- Ethena's docs do not state order types, algos, or slippage assumptions; the Ethena FAQ (Notion) was not fetched.
- No named-firm legging-loss post-mortem exists publicly as far as this search found.
