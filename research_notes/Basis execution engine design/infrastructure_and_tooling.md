## KQ1. Venue latency and colocation: where the matching engines are, what RTTs are reported, and how to handle two legs in different regions

### Takeaway
Five of the six target venues (Binance, Bitget, Gate, Hyperliquid, plus most Asian second-tier CEXs) sit in AWS Tokyo ap-northeast-1; Bybit sits in AWS Singapore (apse1-az2/az3); OKX sits on Alibaba Cloud Hong Kong. Co-located REST/WS round-trips are single-digit ms on the same cloud/AZ (~3-8 ms Tokyo->Binance, ~3 ms Tokyo->Hyperliquid API), while Tokyo<->Singapore is ~65-68 ms and Tokyo->Europe ~125-245 ms, so a Binance/Bybit or Binance/OKX basis trade necessarily carries a ~35-70 ms cross-region hedge lag unless you run one engine instance per region and let the harder leg lead.

How firms handle two-region legs
- Legging risk framing: "if the legs of a pair do not execute simultaneously, the trader is exposed to legging risk"; "the economic cost of legging risk is material across a range of common pairs." — [OneChronos: Pairs Without Legging Risk](https://www.onechronos.com/insights/pairs-part-one/)
- Practitioner pattern (educational repo, paper-trading only): "the harder leg leads, the hedging leg always crosses, partial fills resize the second leg to what actually filled, and a timeout re-queries by client order id rather than re-submitting blind." — [pranay123-stack/delta-exchange-crypto-algorithmic-trading](https://github.com/pranay123-stack/delta-exchange-crypto-algorithmic-trading)
- Vendor claim: "with exchange latency at 200-400ms, execution speed is the thin line between profit and a 'legged' trade" for cross-venue arb from a non-colocated host (snippet only). — [godzilla.dev vs Hummingbot](https://dev.to/godzilla_dev/hummingbot-vs-godzilladev-an-honest-comparison-for-funding-rate-arbitrage-and-market-making-2026-58il)

### Inferences
- For a Binance/Bitget/Gate/Hyperliquid basket the whole engine can live in one AWS Tokyo AZ; only Bybit (Singapore) and OKX (Hong Kong) force a cross-region decision. Tokyo->Singapore is fixed at ~65 ms by physics; a dedicated circuit buys only ~3 ms there (vs ~90 ms savings Tokyo->Europe), so for Tokyo/Singapore pairs the money is better spent on AZ selection and stack tuning than on Avelacom-style links.
- The standard design is "region-local gateways + one global coordinator": market-data + order gateway processes in each region next to the venue, a central strategy/hedger that tolerates ~70 ms one-way information lag, and a "lead with the harder/less-liquid leg, cross with the hedge leg" sequencing so that the leg exposed to the lag is the liquid one. Report/finalize state after the hedge leg acks ("hedge-first-then-report").
- For an execution algo (not a market maker), the cross-region lag mainly manifests as hedge-lag cost (mid moves during 70-150 ms) and stale-basis risk, not as queue-priority loss; that cost is usually a fraction of a bp on BTC/ETH and grows with volatility.
- Arbitron-style REST RTTs (16-35 ms) include TLS and application overhead; a persistent WS/FIX session from a co-located, tuned host should see order acks in the low single-digit ms on Binance/Bybit (cf. the 3-8 ms claim), but I found no independently measured 2025-2026 order-ack distribution for each venue.

- No primary Binance/OKX/Gate statement of the exact AZ ID of their matching engines (Bybit is the only venue that publishes AZ IDs). Binance dev-community threads on latency reduction were 451-blocked.
- No measured order-ack (submit->ack) latency distributions from co-located hosts for Binance, Bybit, OKX, Bitget, Gate in 2025-2026; the Oxford paper contains them but full text was inaccessible.
- Whether OKX still runs fully on Alibaba Cloud HK vs a hybrid AWS deployment could not be confirmed from primary sources.
- The Bybit "Ultra-low latency" program: no page found under that name; only the AZ IDs in the FAQ and the general help-center article.

## KQ2. Protocols: WS vs REST vs FIX, rate limits, market-data feeds, Hyperliquid API constraints

### Takeaway
Binance is the only target venue with a public FIX API (spot only: order entry, drop copy, market data; Ed25519 keys; 10,000 msgs/10 s); every venue offers WebSocket order entry that shares rate limits with REST, and WS is the practical low-latency order path. For market data, the fastest feeds are Binance bookTicker (real-time BBO) / SBE streams, Bybit orderbook.1 (10 ms) and orderbook.50 (20 ms), Bitget books1 (1 ms)/books5 (10 ms), and Binance diff-depth at 100 ms (spot; changelog shows depth update speed pushed to 50 -> 25 -> 20 ms during 2025-2026). Hyperliquid caps at 1200 REST weight/min per IP, 2000 WS messages/min, 100 in-flight posts, address-based limits tied to USDC traded, and has ~200 ms median / 900 ms p99 end-to-end order latency for co-located clients (Glassnode measured ~884 ms median in Mar 2026).

### Inferences
- For an execution algo, WS order entry with a persistent authenticated session (Binance WS API with Ed25519 `session.logon`, Bybit `/v5/trade`, OKX WS `order`) is the right default; FIX only adds value on Binance spot and mainly for firms that already have a FIX OMS. The measurable FIX advantage over WS on Binance is undocumented.
- Market data: subscribe to the BBO stream (bookTicker / orderbook.1 / books1 / bbo) for the hedge trigger and to a 100 ms-or-faster diff-depth stream for depth-aware sizing; the BBO feed leads L2 by a few ms on Binance futures. SBE (Binance) and Bitget SBE Depth50 are the lowest-overhead options for a Rust/C++ decoder.
- Rate budgets are generous relative to an execution algo's needs (10-20 orders/s/UID on Bybit is the tightest of the set; a slicing algo rarely exceeds 1-5 orders/s per symbol), but cancel-replace loops during volatility can hit Bybit's 10/s linear limit; batch endpoints and amend-in-place should be preferred.
- Hyperliquid's ~0.2-0.9 s (measured up to ~0.9 s median) fill latency is 2 orders of magnitude slower than CEX acks; it should be the "hard/lead" leg in a CEX-vs-Hyperliquid basis trade, with the CEX hedge fired on Hyperliquid fill confirmation, and the address-based limit (1 request per USDC traded) makes tiny test orders and high cancel churn expensive for new addresses.
- The Binance changelog's 50/25/20 ms depth cadence changes (late 2025-2026) suggest the "100ms" naming is now stale; verify the actual push interval empirically per stream.

## KQ3. Architecture patterns and internal latency budgets

### Takeaway
Pure HFT designs (thread-per-core, lock-free SPSC queues, kernel bypass, pre-allocated memory) target ~50 us tick-to-trade, but every measured external hop in crypto is milliseconds (3-8 ms co-located ack, 65 ms cross-region, 100-900 ms Hyperliquid). For an execution algo, a 1-10 ms internal budget with a single-threaded event loop per venue, a Rust/C++ hot path (or Rust core + Python control plane as Nautilus/godzilla do) and framework-clock-based timestamps is sufficient; sub-millisecond engineering only pays for market making inside a shared placement group.

### Cited Findings
- Rust low-latency design (Sep 2024): thread-per-core with `isolcpus`/`nohz_full`/`rcu_nocbs`, context switch cost 10-50 us; SPSC queue 15-30 ns push/pop vs 50-200 ns uncontended mutex; kernel TCP stack 50-200 us vs OpenOnload 5-10 us vs DPDK 1-5 us; malloc 100-500 ns => pre-allocate pools, never allocate in the hot path; example 50 us budget: network rx 5 / parse 3 / book update 8 / strategy 10 / order build 4 / tx 5 / jitter reserve 15 us; measured DPDK RTT 3.2 us vs kernel TCP 52 us; governor=performance, turbo off, IRQ pinning. — [mechanicalsnail: Engineering Low-Latency Trading Systems in Rust](https://mechanicalsnail.com/posts/low-latency-trading-rust/)
- Rust channel patterns for trading: lock-free ring buffers between pinned cores; spsc/mpsc/mpmc channels (snippet only). — [markrbest: HFT and Rust](https://markrbest.github.io/hft-and-rust/); [rustquant.dev initial plan](https://rustquant.dev/blog/0-initial-plan/)
- godzilla.dev (vendor, self-declared bias): C++ execution core + Python strategy layer via pybind11, process-isolated market-data / strategy / execution with a journal-based shared-memory event path (built on open-source Kungfu runtime); local software path 121-135 us median, 435-662 us p99 on a laptop-class i7-1360P; "no network, no exchange, no fills." — [dev.to comparison, 2026](https://dev.to/godzilla_dev/hummingbot-vs-godzilladev-an-honest-comparison-for-funding-rate-arbitrage-and-market-making-2026-58il)
- NautilusTrader: Rust core (matching, order books, clocks, message bus), Python strategy API; "same strategy code runs in backtest and live"; production field report pitfalls: never set backtest latency to zero ("use 5-200ms realistic values"), use the framework clock not system time, misconfigured Redis persistence loses state on restart; instrument tick-size errors cause "silent, expensive bugs"; ~3 weeks to productivity. — [Hasan Javed field report, 2026-03-19](https://hasanjaved.me/blog/nautilus-trader-production-field-report/); [nautilus_trader GitHub](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader)
- Hummingbot: Python + Cython, single `Clock` ticking "every second by default", connectors update before strategies on each tick; order tracker starts tracking "before it is submitted to the exchange API" so timeouts don't lose orders; DEX access via Docker Gateway. — [Hummingbot Architecture Part 1](https://hummingbot.org/blog/hummingbot-architecture---part-1/)
- Cross-region stack: P99 within 0.1-0.3 ms of P50 and jitter <90 us achievable on dedicated circuits, i.e., cross-region variance is small relative to the ~65 ms mean. — [AWS/Avelacom](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/ultra-low-latency-cross-region-crypto-trading-with-avelacom-and-aws/)
- Bitget supports "microsecond gateway timestamps", useful for one-way latency attribution. — [Bitget Academy](https://www.bitget.com/academy/what-is-bitget-average-api-latency-for-institutional-order-execution)
- Logging/TCA data model reference: QuestDB + Polars tutorial computing VWAP, slippage and markout from tick data. — [QuestDB TCA post](https://questdb.com/blog/transaction-cost-analysis-questdb-polars/)

### Inferences
- Tick-to-trade budget: because the smallest external hop is ~3-8 ms and the cross-region hop is ~65 ms, an internal decision latency of 1-10 ms is invisible in TCA for an execution algo. Sub-100 us matters only if you are competing for queue position (market making) or racing a specific event; it is not required here.
- Recommended shape: one process (or one Tokio/async runtime) per venue region running (a) WS market-data decoder -> local order book, (b) order gateway (WS/FIX session, rate-limit governor, idempotent client-order-id map), each single-threaded with lock-free queues to (c) a strategy/hedger core; a Python control plane (config, monitoring, TCA) sits outside the hot path. This is the Nautilus/godzilla/Kungfu shape.
- Clock discipline: stamp every inbound message with local monotonic time plus the exchange timestamp (`E`/`T` on Binance, `ts` on Bybit/OKX, gateway microsecond timestamps on Bitget), NTP/chrony-sync hosts (PTP is unnecessary at ms budgets), and record submit/ack/fill timestamps per client-order-id into an append-only journal (Parquet/QuestDB) so hedge lag and feed lag can be reconstructed post-trade.
- Language: Rust (hftbacktest, Nautilus, Barter) or C++ for the gateway and book; Python (asyncio) for orchestration is standard and adequate for a 1-10 ms budget. Pure Python hot paths (Hummingbot's 1 s tick) are too coarse for hedge timing.

### Gaps
- No public engineering blog from Wintermute, GSR, Keyrock, Auros, Portofino or Cumberland describing internal execution-engine architecture with numbers was found in this pass; the only architecture posts with numbers are generic HFT (Rust) or vendor comparisons.
- No published measured "tick-to-trade" distribution for a crypto execution algo (vs market maker); the 1-10 ms adequacy claim is an inference from venue latencies.
- LMAX Disruptor / Jane Street / HRT references were not fetched (out of tool budget); they are general HFT references and not crypto-specific.

## KQ4. Risk and safety design

### Takeaway
The recurring practitioner patterns are: bounded unhedged delta with the hedge leg always crossing, write-ahead persistence of the execution state machine so a restart can distinguish "submitted, outcome unknown" from "never sent", re-query by client order id on timeout instead of blind resubmission, reconciliation per venue+symbol aggregate that flags (never absorbs) surplus positions, kill switches that verify flat rather than assume it, and framework-level order-rate governors and trading-state modes (ACTIVE/HALTED/REDUCING) as implemented in Nautilus.

### Cited Findings
- Two-leg execution reference (paper-trading, educational): 16-state (hedging) and 23-state (basis arb) execution machines with "write-ahead transition persistence" so restart recovery can "distinguish submitted-but-unknown outcomes from never-submitted orders"; "the harder leg leads, the hedging leg always crosses, partial fills resize the second leg to what actually filled"; timeout => "re-queries by client order id rather than re-submitting blind"; reconciliation "per venue+symbol aggregate rather than per leg, because a venue reports one net position for several cycles; a surplus is flagged rather than absorbed"; kill switch "verifies liquidation actually reached flat rather than assuming success"; refuses startup if safety flags are disabled; position limits "computed rather than asserted" from contract specs; risk measured on basis-spread covariance not asset covariance. Author states "No live-traded track record is published." — [pranay123-stack/delta-exchange-crypto-algorithmic-trading](https://github.com/pranay123-stack/delta-exchange-crypto-algorithmic-trading)
- NautilusTrader RiskEngine: price/quantity precision checks, `max_notional_per_order`, cash balance impact, order-rate limit enforcement, trading states ACTIVE / HALTED / REDUCING; OMS types NETTING / HEDGING; live reconciliation detects local-vs-venue position discrepancies and supports `OrderFillVoided` corrections. — [Nautilus execution concepts](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/execution/)
- Hummingbot tracks an order "before it is submitted" so an API timeout cannot orphan an accepted order. — [Hummingbot Architecture](https://hummingbot.org/blog/hummingbot-architecture---part-1/)
- Bybit WS trade: an ack "indicates that the request was successfully accepted" only; order state must come from the private order stream. — [Bybit WS trade guideline](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/websocket/trade/guideline)
- Binance: violating rate limits escalates to IP bans of 2 min to 3 days => a rate governor is a safety control, not just an optimization. — [Binance rest-api.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/binance/binance-spot-api-docs/master/rest-api.md)
- Hyperliquid: address throttled to 1 request / 10 s if the USDC-traded allowance is exhausted; cancels get extra allowance (min(limit+100k, 2x)); congestion mode limits an address to 2x its prior-day maker share; server may disconnect WS without notice. — [Hyperliquid rate limits](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/rate-limits-and-user-limits); [WS docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/websocket)
- Exchange downtime is real and cloud-correlated: OKX outage from Alibaba Cloud equipment failure. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/okx-cites-intermittent-outage-amid-alibaba-cloud-equipment-anomaly)
- Marketable limit orders on Bybit/Binance have a "substantial probability of failing to execute immediately" under latency/volatility (i.e., the hedge leg can miss if sent as a non-crossing limit). — [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:cdab1de2-7576-42e2-abae-ab12371eba76)
- Exchange ADL treats hedged positions preferentially: "Hedged parts are ranked lower than all unhedged positions in the ADL queue" (snippet only; exchange help page). — [OKX delta-neutral help](https://www.okx.com/en-us/help/introduction-to-delta-neutral-strategy)

### Inferences
- Max-unhedged-delta limit should be expressed in notional and in time (e.g., "no more than X USD unhedged for more than N x expected hedge RTT"); with Tokyo/Singapore legs, N x 65-150 ms is the natural unit; with Hyperliquid, allow ~1-2 s.
- Cancel-replace races: prefer amend-in-place (Bybit/OKX/Binance futures support amend) and single-in-flight-per-slice discipline; treat "unknown" (timeout, disconnect mid-request) as "assume live until proven otherwise" and re-query by client order id, which all target venues support.
- Degraded mode per venue: if a venue's WS feed stalls (no heartbeat / sequence gap) or its order gateway returns 5xx/429, freeze new slices on that venue, keep hedging on the other venue only in the direction that reduces net delta, and switch reconciliation to REST polling; exit only via kill-switch that confirms flat by reading exchange positions.
- Two-venue inconsistency (local book says hedged, venue says not): the "flag, don't absorb" rule from the reference repo is the safe default; auto-absorbing hides operational bugs and mis-attributes P&L.

### Gaps
- No public numbers from institutional desks on the unhedged-delta thresholds or kill-switch trip levels they actually use.
- No primary source describing how large crypto MMs run "degraded mode" during exchange outages; only anecdotal outage reports.

## KQ6. Transaction cost analysis for crypto execution

### Takeaway
Crypto TCA uses the same benchmarks as TradFi (arrival/implementation shortfall vs a 1-second top-of-book mid, interval VWAP/TWAP, effective spread, markouts) plus crypto-specific items (fee tier, maker ratio, consolidated-book construction because there is no tape). Reported numbers: Talos' $1B / 1,000+ TWAP parent-order study shows ~13 bps arrival underperformance against a 42 bps adverse trend with mild VWAP/TWAP outperformance and 75% maker fills; Anboto reports -0.25 bps TWAP and -0.58 bps arrival slippage vs TradFi TWAP -1 to -2 bps and arrival -10 to -15 bps (vendor figures).

### Cited Findings
- Talos (Apr 2025) metric definitions: Arrival Price = "median 1-second mid-point top-of-book quoted price at the parent order submission time"; Average Execution Price inclusive of maker/taker fees; Market TWAP/VWAP = interval benchmarks over the parent duration on a consolidated book. Case study: 1,000+ parent orders, $1B notional via TWAP: ~13 bps arrival slippage (underperformance), 42 bps average adverse market move, mild outperformance vs TWAP/VWAP, 75% maker fills. Recommendations: shorten duration in adverse trends, switch to POV (5% -> 8-9%) when trend-following, ML slippage attribution. — [Talos TCA insights](https://www.talos.com/insights/execution-insights-through-transaction-cost-analysis-tca-benchmarks-and-slippage)
- Anboto Labs (vendor, undated Medium post): arrival slippage compares execution to prevailing mid at placement; TWAP slippage vs TWAP over the active period; Anboto TWAP slippage -0.25 bps vs "typical TradFi TWAP slippage of -1 to -2 bps"; arrival slippage -0.58 bps vs TradFi -10 to -15 bps (snippet only; page returned 403). — [Anboto Labs](https://medium.com/@anboto_labs/slippage-benchmarks-and-beyond-transaction-cost-analysis-tca-in-crypto-trading-2f0b0186980e)
- CoinRoutes: TCA "captures slippage, market impact, and timing beyond visible trading fees"; post-trade analytics compute effective spread and implementation shortfall; crypto has "no consolidated tape" so TCA is "both harder and more valuable" (snippet only). — [CoinRoutes TCA](https://coinroutes.com/academy/transaction-cost-analysis-tca-digital-assets/)
- CoinAPI: execution-quality measurement in crypto centres on slippage vs arrival and interval VWAP, liquidity depth at decision time, and best-execution across fragmented venues (snippet only). — [CoinAPI blog](https://www.coinapi.io/blog/execution-quality-in-crypto)
- Academic: traditional cost models "systematically underestimate costs during periods of market stress, order book fragmentation, and regime transition" in crypto. — [Entropy 2026 (MDPI)](https://doi.org/10.3390/e28060705)
- Latency-driven slippage on Bybit/Binance market orders is measurable and correlated with volatility/latency/liquidity; authors propose models for fill-failure probability and price degradation to reconcile backtest vs live. — [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:cdab1de2-7576-42e2-abae-ab12371eba76)
- Tooling: QuestDB + Polars recipe for VWAP, slippage and markout on tick data. — [QuestDB](https://questdb.com/blog/transaction-cost-analysis-questdb-polars/); KX/kdb positioning for real-time slippage monitoring (vendor). — [KX blog](https://kx.com/blog/speed-beats-slippage-crypto-market-volatility/)

### Inferences
- For a delta-neutral engine the TCA should be computed on the pair, not the legs: basis-arrival slippage (executed basis vs basis mid at decision), hedge-lag cost (mid move on the hedge venue between lead-leg fill and hedge fill, in bps, with the 65-150 ms cross-region lag as the expected floor), per-leg spread capture (maker vs taker share), fee cost by venue tier, and reject/partial-fill rates; each requires the timestamp journal described in KQ3.
- Benchmarks: single-leg arrival slippage of a few bps for large TWAP orders (Talos ~13 bps under a strong trend; vendor sub-1 bps in calm markets) brackets what to expect; hedge-lag cost on BTC/ETH at ~100 ms is typically well under 1 bp except in volatility spikes, but I found no published number for it.

### Gaps
- No published venue-by-venue benchmark slippage tables (e.g., "BTCUSDT perp on Binance: X bps for $1M taker") from a neutral source; all numbers are vendor case studies.
- No published "hedge lag cost" statistics for CEX basis trades.
- Anboto page could not be opened; its numbers rest on the search snippet.
