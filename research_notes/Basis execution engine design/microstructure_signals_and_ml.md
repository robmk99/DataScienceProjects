# Order-Book Microstructure Signals and ML/RL for Trade Execution in Crypto

Scope: what short-horizon signals and which ML/RL techniques are worth building into a low-latency crypto execution engine, with reported numbers and an explicit split between "proven in production / live experiments" and "academic / backtest only". Crypto evidence prioritized; equities evidence used where crypto evidence is missing.

Legend used throughout: **[LIVE]** = live trading experiment or production deployment claim; **[BT]** = backtest/simulation only; **[EQ]** = equities/futures evidence, not crypto.

---

## Key Question 1: Order book signals — what predictive power is reported (OFI, microprice/queue imbalance, trade flow, multi-level depth, slope, VPIN, liquidations/funding)?

### Takeaway
Order flow imbalance (OFI) at the touch is the single most robust short-horizon signal in both equities and crypto: contemporaneous R² of ~65% at 10 s in US equities (Cont-Kukanov-Stoikov) and ~36% at 1 s / ~57% at 10 s on Binance BTCUSDT in a recent self-recorded replication; SHAP analyses on Binance perps over 2022-2025 rank OFI #1 across large- and small-cap coins. Depth/queue imbalance gives ~0.53-0.73 binary hit rate at 100 ms-1 s on Bybit BTCUSDT depending on book depth used. VPIN, liquidations and funding are lower-frequency (minutes to hours) regime/toxicity signals, not sub-second timing signals; I found no crypto study reporting sub-second predictive power for them.

### Cited Findings

**OFI (Cont, Kukanov, Stoikov 2014) — [EQ] baseline numbers**
- OFI is defined as net change in bid queue minus net change in ask queue at the best quotes (market sells and cancelled bids treated identically). Regressing 10-second mid-price changes on contemporaneous OFI over 50 US stocks (TAQ, April 2010) gives **average R² = 65%, range 35%-79%**, most stocks 60-75%. Trade imbalance alone gives R² = 32% and becomes insignificant once OFI is included. Price-impact coefficient is inversely proportional to depth: β = c/AD^λ with λ ≈ 1. — [Cont, Kukanov, Stoikov, "The price impact of order book events" (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.6402); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1712822)
- Note: these are *contemporaneous* R² values (explaining the same-interval move), not forward forecasts; forward R² is far lower (see gaps).

**OFI in crypto — [BT] with self-recorded Binance L2**
- On self-recorded Binance BTCUSDT L2 (2.8 h, 448k messages), OFI explains **36% of 1-second mid changes (β = 5.9e-4 ticks/lot) and 57% of 10-second changes (β = 6.9e-4)**; R² rises to 0.41 in high-volatility regimes. — [m-ichaels/lob-execution-sim README](https://github.com/m-ichaels/lob-execution-sim) (open-source project; small sample; treat as indicative, not peer-reviewed)

**OFI and trade-flow on Binance perps, multi-year — [BT] academic**
- Binance Futures perpetuals (BTC, LTC, ETC, ENJ, ROSE), 1-second data, Jan 2022-Oct 2025, target = 3-second log return. SHAP ranks **order flow imbalance #1 predictor for all five assets**; the other dominant families are bid-ask spread (attenuates predictability; effect largely monotone with concavity at extremes), VWAP-deviation-from-mid, and signed trade flow / net volume. Feature rankings are highly correlated across assets ("universal, scale-invariant microstructure representation"). Taker strategy on the signal: BTC annualized return 0.13 / IR 0.25; ETC 5.78 / 8.97; ENJ 4.06 / 6.58; ROSE 7.00 / 5.28 (only ETC/ENJ/ROSE significant at p<0.05). Maker strategy on the same signal underperformed and suffered "catastrophic losses" in the 10 Oct 2025 flash crash, validating adverse-selection theory. — [Explainable Patterns in Cryptocurrency Microstructure, arXiv 2602.00776](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00776v1)
- Binance BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT futures 2023-2026, top-20 L2 + trade flow, rolling monthly OOS folds: a coarse **pre-event liquidity-state baseline** strongly predicts post-event liquidity regimes; a shallow nonlinear L2 model adds a robust further gain; **order flow adds value only when layered on top of the L2-state model**, not as a replacement. ETH: order flow value consistent across calm/mixed/stressed regimes (largest under stress); BTC: only isolated 5-minute improvements. Authors propose a "state-first" design principle. — [When Does Order Flow Matter?, arXiv 2607.09230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09230)

**Queue / depth imbalance at multiple levels — crypto — [BT]**
- Bybit BTC/USDT, 100 ms snapshots (single day, 30 Jan 2025; ~100k snapshots main analysis, 1M for sequence tests), up to 200 levels. Features: previous mid, L1 imbalance, 5-level aggregate imbalance, weighted-mid changes across depth. **Binary up/down hit rate: 0.53 at 100 ms (5-level book); 0.71-0.73 at 500 ms (40 levels); 0.69-0.72 at 1000 ms (40 levels)**. Three-class (up/flat/down) F1: 0.41-0.42 at 100 ms, 0.52-0.54 at 500 ms, 0.46-0.54 at 1 s. Depth matters: XGBoost binary accuracy 0.715 with 40 levels vs 0.584 (10 levels) vs 0.580 (5 levels). XGBoost and logistic regression matched or beat DeepLOB/CNN-LSTM by 1-2 pts; Savitzky-Golay smoothing of the label improved all models by 5-10 pts. — [Better Inputs Matter More Than Stacking Another Hidden Layer, arXiv 2506.05764](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05764v2)
- Caveat: the label smoothing and single-day sample inflate these hit rates relative to what a live engine will see on raw mid-price changes.

**Microprice (Stoikov 2018) — [EQ] origin, crypto use**
- Microprice = mid adjusted by imbalance and spread, constructed as a martingale; Stoikov shows empirically it beats mid and weighted-mid as a short-term predictor. — [Stoikov, The Micro-Price (SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2970694); [Quantitative Finance 18(12)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14697688.2018.1489139)
- A Stanford student report on crypto found microprice "the strongest price-change sign predictor, being right about 40% of the time" (3-class). — [Stanford MS&E 448 report](https://web.stanford.edu/class/msande448/2021/Final_reports/gr1.pdf) (student work; low weight)
- hftbacktest's reference market-making example on Binance Futures BTC/USDT and ETH/USDT uses a standardized multi-level depth imbalance (sum of bid qty minus ask qty within a depth window, standardized over a rolling 1 h window) as the alpha: `fair_price = mid + c1 * alpha` (c1 = 160 for BTC), then skews quotes off a reservation price. Backtest with real order-latency data and 0.005% maker rebate: BTC/USDT May 2023 Sharpe 10.83, return 34.24%, 0.0139% per trade; Feb 2025 Sharpe 5.37, return 45.96%, 0.0086% per trade — per-trade edge declining over time. — [hftbacktest: Market Making with Alpha - Order Book Imbalance](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/Market%20Making%20with%20Alpha%20-%20Order%20Book%20Imbalance.html)

**Imbalance as a fill/adverse-selection signal — Binance BTCUSDT perps — [LIVE]**
- Live experiment: 232,897 minimum-size maker orders on Binance BTCUSDT perp over one week (Feb 2024), 127,051 fills. Fill probability is almost fully explained by near-side queue size and opposite-side queue size at submission (**OLS R² = 0.946**); fill probability ranges from ~30% (large near, small opposite) to >90% (small near, large opposite). **Negative correlation between fill likelihood and post-fill return**: 1-second markouts of -0.058 bp (front of queue, favorable imbalance) to -0.775 bp (back of queue), -0.5 to -0.76 bp when near side small / opposite large. Imbalance-based taker strategy: +1 bp pre-fee, **-1.96 bp after 1.5 bp round-trip taker fee**; imbalance-based maker strategies -0.47 to -0.49 bp/trade; naive market making Sharpe -109. — [The Market Maker's Dilemma, arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)

**Order book slope, multi-level depth (equities/crypto stylized facts)**
- Deep-in-book orders generate measurable price impact (contradicting top-of-book-only models); suspicious large orders sit on average 6.85 bp from mid vs 1.03 bp for normal large orders (Coinbase BTC-USD/ETH-USD). — [Spoofability of LOBs, arXiv 2504.15908](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15908v1)

**VPIN — crypto**
- VAR study on high-frequency Bitcoin data: VPIN significantly predicts future price jumps; both VPIN and jump size show positive serial correlation; time-zone and day-of-week effects in VPIN. No sub-second horizon; it is a toxicity/jump-risk regime indicator. — [Bitcoin wild moves: order flow toxicity and price jumps, Research in International Business and Finance 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0275531925004192)

**Longer-horizon order-imbalance predictability in crypto futures**
- Opening (quarter-hour) order imbalance predicts returns over 4-12 h with R² of only **0.046% (4 h) to 0.176% (12 h)** — orders of magnitude weaker than sub-second OFI. — [The Quarter-Hour Effect, arXiv 2607.09426](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)

**Funding / liquidations**
- Funding is a periodic long/short transfer that pulls the perp toward index; practitioner commentary notes 8-hour funding cycles "create predictable intraday patterns in spreads and depth" and that stretched funding can precede snapbacks. — [MetaMask: perpetual funding frequency and strategies](https://metamask.io/news/perpetual-futures-funding-frequency-strategies); theory in [Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures, arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v7)

### Inferences
- For a sub-second execution engine, the useful signal stack is: (1) L1 OFI (event-driven), (2) multi-level depth/queue imbalance (10-40 levels, standardized over a rolling window), (3) signed trade-flow over 100 ms-5 s windows, (4) spread state as a gate. This matches what appears in both the SHAP study and the hftbacktest reference strategy, and is what the Schnaubelt PPO agent learned to rely on (see KQ4).
- Reported hit rates of 0.70+ at 500 ms-1 s on Bybit use smoothed labels and 40-level books; expect raw-label, live hit rates closer to 0.55-0.60, and expect the edge to be smaller than one taker fee (the Binance live experiment shows +1 bp pre-fee edge vs 1.5 bp round-trip fee). The signal's value for an executing (not alpha-seeking) engine is in *timing and sizing* child orders, not in generating standalone P&L.
- The 2607.09230 result (liquidity-state first, order flow as a layered refinement) argues that the engine's scheduler should condition on a coarse liquidity/volatility regime before using OFI for micro-timing.
- VPIN, liquidation cascades and funding should be treated as regime/risk gates (widen participation limits, pause passive posting during toxic flow), not as ms-scale signals.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed crypto study reporting forward (not contemporaneous) R² of OFI at 100 ms-1 s horizons on Binance/Bybit perps; the 36%/57% Binance numbers come from a small open-source project.
- No source found quantifying the predictive value of recent liquidation prints (e.g., Binance forceOrder stream) for sub-minute returns.
- No source found for order-book *slope* predictive power in crypto specifically.

## Key Question 2: Liquidity estimation — real vs displayed liquidity (spoofing, icebergs, hidden refill), resilience/refill after sweeps, sweep-cost curves, child-order sizing from depth

### Takeaway
Displayed depth overstates executable liquidity: a Coinbase L3 study finds 31% of large orders meet a spoofability criterion and that spoof-like orders sit ~6.85 bp from mid, and industry sources report 40-70% of visible limit orders never execute. Metaorder impact on Bitcoin follows the square-root law over four decades of size, so sweep-cost curves are concave and child-order size should be set from square-root impact plus the observed post-sweep refill dynamics (t^(1/3) recovery-front scaling), not from displayed depth alone.

### Cited Findings
- **Spoofing / non-bona-fide liquidity (Coinbase BTC-USD, ETH-USD L3, Dec 2022 train / Dec 2024 test, 88.3M orders, 8.6M "large" ≥ $4,500):** 31% of large orders satisfy an expected-profit spoofability criterion. Suspicious large orders: 0% posted at best bid/ask vs 4.2% for normal large orders; mean distance from mid 6.85 bp vs 1.03 bp. Model: single-hidden-layer (64 unit) probabilistic NN predicting conditional mid-move distribution from Hawkes-style order-flow features (3 time scales x 4 distance scales), Box-Cox normalized inputs. Conclusion: "displayed liquidity at narrow price levels substantially overestimates accessible liquidity during manipulation events"; posting distance must be a feature of any real-liquidity model. — [arXiv 2504.15908](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15908v1)
- Industry commentary: "between 40% and 70% of visible limit orders on major exchanges never execute — they get canceled before being filled"; one tracked session had 55% of resting orders within 1% of mid cancelled within 30 s. — [Kalena blog](https://blog.kalena.ai/crypto-order-book-anatomy-the-definitive-mechanical-breakdown-of-how-orders-stack-match-and-move-price-in-2026) (vendor blog; unverified methodology)
- **Icebergs on crypto venues:** supported natively on Binance (incl. Futures), Kraken, Gate.io; detection relies on refill patterns (maker fill followed within a short delay by a new order at the same side/price), volume-traded-at-level vs max displayed depth, and tape analysis. Open-source refill-chain detector: [ob-analytics PR #271](https://github.com/mczielinski/ob-analytics/pull/271), [issue #111](https://github.com/mczielinski/ob-analytics/issues/111); method descriptions in [Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/blog/advanced-order-flow-trading-spotting-hidden-liquidity-iceberg-orders) and [ClearEdge](https://www.clearedge.trading/post/detecting-iceberg-orders-hidden-liquidity-futures). Peer-reviewed iceberg prediction exists for CME (Zotikov/Devexperts) — [arXiv 1909.09495](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.09495) [EQ].
- **Sweep-cost curve / market impact on Bitcoin:** over one million metaorders on a Bitcoin/USD exchange confirm the **square-root impact law with exponent ≈ 0.5 across four decades of metaorder size**, holding along the whole trajectory of the metaorder, and despite the near-absence of stat-arb/HFT at the time; decomposing flow shows the "uninformed" component decays almost completely long-term. — [Donier & Bonart, A Million Metaorder Analysis of Market Impact on the Bitcoin, arXiv 1412.4503](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.4503); survey [Said, Market Impact: Empirical Evidence, Theory and Practice, arXiv 2205.07385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.07385)
- **Resilience / refill after aggressive trades:** empirical and simulated recovery front after aggressive liquidity taking scales as **t^(1/3)**; the true replenishment time-scale is at the event level (1-20 book updates after the shock); single-tick spreads produce asymmetric replenishment. — [LOB resiliency after effective market orders, arXiv 1602.00731](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00731) [EQ]; Hawkes cross-excitation as a resiliency model in [Hawkes processes in finance, arXiv 1502.04592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04592)
- **Crypto-specific LOB stylized facts:** hourly average liquidity costs differ largely across crypto exchanges and intraday patterns known from equities are largely absent. — [Testing Stylized Facts of Bitcoin Limit Order Books, JRFM 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm12010025) (full text not retrievable; abstract-level claim)
- **RL-Exec impact model (BTC-USD replay):** replay-with-impact environment applies endogenous transient impact with exponential resilience, partial fills, maker/taker fees and latency so the agent "pays" for footprint. — [RL-Exec, arXiv 2511.07434](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07434v1)
- **Multi-level impact conditional on OFI [EQ]:** impact of a market order depends on prevailing OFI; practitioner algos anecdotally use order-flow feedback to place deeper when flow is one-sided. — [Empirical Study of Market Impact Conditional on Order-Flow Imbalance, arXiv 2004.08290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.08290); [Optimal Execution with Dynamic Order Flow Imbalance, arXiv 1409.2618](https://arxiv.org/html/1409.2618v2)

### Inferences
- Child-order sizing rule consistent with the evidence: cap each aggressive child so expected impact (square-root law calibrated per venue/pair) stays below a bps budget, and cap by *discounted* displayed depth, where the discount grows with distance from mid (spoof-prone region ~5-10 bp out) and with recent cancel-rate at that level. Re-size after a sweep only once the level-refill (t^(1/3) front) has been observed, i.e., wait a few book updates rather than a fixed wall-clock time.
- A practical "real liquidity" estimator is a per-level survival/refill model: track cancel rate, time-at-level, refill events after maker fills (iceberg signature), and volume-traded-at-level vs max displayed. This is what the open-source and vendor detectors do; no crypto paper gives a validated accuracy number for it.

### Gaps
- No published quantitative study of order-book resilience/refill time on Binance or Bybit perps (the t^(1/3) law and event-scale replenishment are equity results).
- No crypto-specific accuracy figures for iceberg/hidden-liquidity detectors.
- No public per-venue square-root impact coefficients (Y-factor) for BTC/ETH perps on Binance/Bybit in 2024-2026; the million-metaorder study is from 2014 data on a spot venue.

## Key Question 3: Fill probability / queue models for passive orders and post-vs-cross decisions

### Takeaway
The queue-reactive model (Huang-Lehalle-Rosenbaum 2015) is the standard state-dependent queue model and has been extended with deep learning and order-size effects, but live crypto evidence shows (a) fill probability at the touch on Binance is ~0.95 R²-explainable by near/opposite queue sizes, (b) fill likelihood and post-fill return are negatively correlated (winner's curse), (c) on Binance touch orders fill mostly because the quote jumps through them, not because the queue drains, and (d) L2-based queue-position models overestimate queue-ahead by ~20-29% and naive replay roughly doubles short-horizon fill probability. The post-vs-cross decision must therefore price the adverse selection of a fill, not just its probability.

### Cited Findings
- **Queue-reactive model:** LOB as a Markov queuing system around a reference price; arrival intensities of limit/cancel/market orders depend on the state of the queues (not just best queue positions). Published JASA 2015. — [Huang, Lehalle, Rosenbaum, arXiv 1312.0563](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563); extensions: order sizes [arXiv 2405.18594](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.18594), deep-learning QR simulator [arXiv 2501.08822](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.08822), localized QR for exogenous moves [Lehalle project page](http://www.cmap.polytechnique.fr/~charles-albert.lehalle/projects/2024QR/)
- **Fill probability, state-dependent flows / survival analysis:** [Fill Probabilities in a LOB with State-Dependent Stochastic Order Flows, arXiv 2403.02572](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.02572); [KANFormer survival-analysis fill-probability model, arXiv 2512.05734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.05734); queue-position valuation [Moallemi & Yuan 2016](https://moallemi.com/ciamac/papers/queue-value-2016.pdf) [EQ]
- **Live Binance BTCUSDT perp maker experiment (Feb 2024, 232,897 orders):** fill probability ≈ f(near-side queue, opposite-side queue) with R² 0.946; fill prob 30% (large near / small opposite) to >90% (small near / large opposite); 1-s markouts -0.058 bp (front of queue, favorable) to -0.775 bp (back of queue); logistic/random-forest "reversal" classifier on price dynamics (100 ms-5 min windows), trade-volume patterns, queue ratios and recent order flow needed to make high-fill-probability posts profitable. Conclusion: viable maker strategies "often require a contrarian approach"; posting with the imbalance puts you at the back of a big queue with poor returns. — [arXiv 2502.18625](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2) [LIVE]
- **Binance BTCUSDT touch-order fill curves (3 BTC probe, self-recorded L2):** fill probability **0.36 / 0.67 / 0.84 within 5 / 30 / 120 s**; by queue-ahead tercile at 5 s: 0.73 (low) / 0.62 (mid) / 0.53 (high); curve is flat in distance behind the touch — "fills come from the quote jumping through the order, not from the queue draining". **Bitstamp validation (21,687 real touch orders):** naive FIFO overestimates queue-ahead by +21%, PowerProb(n=3) +24%, RiskAverse +29%; L2 models flag ≤17% of actually-filled orders as filled. **Replay bias:** replay fill rates 0.17/0.58/0.75 at 5/30/120 s vs Kaplan-Meier on real orders 0.07/0.30/0.62 (replay ~doubles short-horizon fills). Passive touch fills mark out against you by -400 to -460 ticks at 100 ms and -470 to -1,700 ticks at 60 s; corr(filled, subsequent return) ≈ -0.5 in all simulator modes. — [lob-execution-sim README](https://github.com/m-ichaels/lob-execution-sim) (open-source, small samples, tick units as reported; indicative only)
- **Taker side — live Bybit/Binance probe (3M+ orders on Bybit over one week, 40k market orders on Binance):** actual vs expected outcome (from the last LOB snapshot at submission) diverge because of LOB updates during exchange/trader/network delays; market orders get worsened fills, marketable limit orders have "substantial" failure-to-fill-immediately probability; discrepancies strongly correlated with volatility, latency and LOB liquidity; **adverse selection for takers**: orders that would have been profitable get worse-than-expected fills, unprofitable ones get expected fills. Paper provides parsimonious models of P(fail-to-fill) and fill-price worsening for backtests. — [Albers, Cucuringu, Howison, Shestopaloff, Quantitative Finance 25(6) 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14697688.2025.2515933); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4677989) [LIVE]
- **Crypto RL agent learned post/cross rule (Schnaubelt):** "order placement becomes more aggressive in anticipation of lower execution probabilities, which is indicated by trade and order imbalances"; features that mattered were current liquidity costs and queue imbalances; aggressiveness rises when unfavorable price moves are expected, preferring limit fills to avoid taker fees. — [Schnaubelt, EJOR 2022 / FAU CRIS](https://cris.fau.de/publications/262175107/) [BT]
- **hftbacktest queue models:** RiskAverseQueueModel, PowerProbQueueModel2/3, SquareProbQueueModel, LogProbQueueModel2; the tutorial shows different queue models produce substantially different cumulative returns and that some assets violate the Poisson-arrival assumption; the recommended practice is to pick the model whose backtest matches real fills. — [hftbacktest Probability Queue Models](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/Probability%20Queue%20Models.html)

### Inferences
- Engine rule implied by the live evidence: post only when (expected fill prob x (spread capture + rebate) - expected adverse markout) > (taker fee + expected impact) - and compute the markout term from queue position and imbalance, since a high fill probability post is usually a post into adverse flow. On Binance perps, 1-s markouts for back-of-queue fills (~-0.78 bp) are of the same order as the maker/taker fee difference, so naive "always post" is not free.
- Because Binance touch fills come from the quote crossing you rather than queue draining, sophisticated L3 queue tracking adds less on Binance than on equities; a simple near/opposite queue-size logistic model (R² 0.95) is sufficient for fill probability, and effort is better spent on the adverse-selection (markout) model and on latency-aware fail-to-fill probability for marketable limits.
- Calibrate any queue model to real fills: L2 models overestimate queue-ahead by ~20-30%, so expect backtests with them to *under*-fill relative to live if uncorrected, while naive replay over-fills by ~2x at short horizons.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed time-to-fill distribution for Binance/Bybit perps beyond the two experiments above; no figures for ETH or altcoins.
- The Albers et al. paper's exact percentiles (fail-to-fill %, bps worsening) are behind a paywall; only qualitative findings were retrievable.

## Key Question 4: ML for execution — supervised LOB prediction (GBM, DeepLOB, LSTM/Transformer) and RL for execution: reported improvements vs TWAP/VWAP and failure modes

### Takeaway
Supervised LOB models deliver 0.70-0.73 hit rates at 0.5-1 s on crypto with tree models matching deep nets; the deep-net advantage documented on equities (DeepLOB F1 0.83/0.73/0.80 at 10/20/30 ticks on FI-2010) largely vanishes on crypto once inputs are engineered, and forecasting power is strongly tick-size/spread dependent and weakly actionable. RL execution papers report 2-23 bps over TWAP on crypto and 15-32 pp Sharpe/PnL over heuristics on equities, but nearly all are single-asset, one-month OOS, with parametric or replay impact models; the only production RL execution claims (JPM LOXM ~15% efficiency, Man AHL routing) come without public numbers. The dominant failure modes are simulator bias (replay doubles fills, no permanent impact, no counterparty reaction), non-stationarity (crypto models decay within days-weeks), and reward/benchmark leakage (VWAP proxies using displayed depth).

### Cited Findings

**Supervised LOB prediction**
- DeepLOB (CNN + Inception + LSTM on 100x40 LOB tensor): FI-2010 F1 83.40% / 72.82% / 80.35% at 10/20/30 ticks; "stable out-of-sample" and transfers to unseen instruments. — [Zhang, Zohren, Roberts, arXiv 1808.03668](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.03668) [EQ]
- Temporal CNNs on Coinbase BTC spot LOB: **71% walk-forward accuracy at a 2-second horizon**. — [Ascertaining price formation in cryptocurrency markets with DeepLearning, arXiv 2003.00803](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00803); related [Deep Learning for Digital Asset LOBs, arXiv 2010.01241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01241)
- Bybit BTCUSDT 100 ms: XGBoost/logistic regression match or beat DeepLOB/CNN-LSTM by 1-2 pts; preprocessing + tuning dominate architecture; latency warning that an 80%-accurate model taking 2 s to predict 1 s ahead is "commercially useless". — [arXiv 2506.05764](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05764v2)
- Deep LOB forecasting practicability (15 NASDAQ stocks, 2017-2019, DeepLOB): MCC at 10-update horizon only **0.11 (small-tick) / 0.13 (medium-tick) / 0.29 (large-tick)**; at 100 updates small-tick forecasts are "producing random forecasts"; "high forecasting power does not necessarily correspond to actionable trading signals"; introduces probability of executing complete correct transactions, which *falls* as confidence thresholds rise. — [Briola, Bartolucci, Aste, PMC12315853](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12315853/) [EQ]
- Kolm, Turiel, Westray (115 Nasdaq stocks): models trained on order-flow (OFI-type stationary) inputs significantly outperform models trained on raw LOB; simple off-the-shelf nets suffice; effective forecast horizon ≈ two average price changes; "information-rich" stocks are more predictable. — [Deep Order Flow Imbalance, Mathematical Finance 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mafi.12413); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3900141) [EQ]
- Model decay in crypto LOB models: models "lose predictive power rapidly", with significant decay within days to weeks; proposed forward-gated replacement (shadow evaluation with delayed labels before switching) improves Sharpe/drawdown vs fixed retraining schedules. — [Train Often, Deploy Selectively, arXiv 2607.28577](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28577)

**RL for execution — crypto [BT]**
- Schnaubelt (EJOR 2022): 18 months, several crypto pairs, 300M trades, 3.5M order-book states, virtual limit-order exchange with hand-crafted state features and purpose-built reward; PPO "reliably learns superior order placement strategies" and rediscovers strategies known from established markets; learned policies use liquidity cost and queue/trade imbalances. — [FAU CRIS](https://cris.fau.de/publications/262175107/); [EJOR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221721003854). A search summary reported PPO reduces average execution cost by "roughly 2 bps" vs TWAP — I could not verify this figure in the abstract; treat as unconfirmed.
- RL-Exec (PPO, SB3, 256/128 MLP; BTC-USD 20-level LOB at 1 s; train Jan 2020, test Feb 2020; replay-with-transient-impact, fees, latency, partial fills; sell-only liquidation): vs TWAP and a depth-weighted VWAP proxy, **+2.25-2.68 bps at 30 min [CI +0.95, +4.05], +7.6-7.7 bps at 60 min [+4.7, +10.8], +23.0 bps at 120 min [+18.0, +28.5]**, Wilcoxon + BH-FDR over 27 test days. Limitations stated: single asset/venue, sell-only, parametric impact, VWAP comparator uses displayed depth not traded volume, one-month OOS, no walk-forward. — [arXiv 2511.07434](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07434v1)
- Deep-learning VWAP allocation (Binance perps BTC/ETH/BNB/ADA/XRP, hourly, 2020-2024, Temporal Linear Network minimizing VWAP slippage directly): absolute VWAP loss BTC 0.159 -> 0.120, ETH 0.178 -> 0.139, ADA 0.226 -> 0.188, XRP 0.224 -> 0.182 vs naive flat allocation; assumes negligible impact, static curve. — [arXiv 2502.13722](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13722v2)
- Hobbyist DQN on crypto reported 37.71% shortfall reduction for 10 BTC and 26.95% for 20 BTC vs immediate execution — not peer-reviewed. — [QuantifiedTrader project](https://quantifiedtrader.com/projects/optimal-execution-rl-agent/)
- Actor-critic RL with both market and limit orders vs TWAP/Almgren-Chriss (Cheridito & Weiss, code at github.com/moritzweiss/rlte); numbers not extractable from PDF. — [arXiv 2507.06345](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06345)
- "Large Execution Models" (Genet & Inzirillo 2025): neural execution policies vs TWAP/VWAP; academic, no production claims. — [arXiv 2509.25211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25211)

**RL for execution — equities/futures [BT] [EQ]**
- Nevmyvaka, Feng, Kearns (ICML 2006): 1.5 years of ms-scale NASDAQ LOB data; "low-impact" state-space factorization with private (inventory, time) and market variables. — [PDF](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/papers/rlexec.pdf) (improvement percentages not extractable from the PDF here; see gaps)
- Ning, Lin, Jaimungal (DDQN, fully connected net, experience replay; LOB features + signals; hourly PnL vs TWAP on 9 stocks): DDQN "outperforms" TWAP and reaches theoretical optimum in simulated settings. Published Applied Mathematical Finance 28 (2021) 361-380. — [arXiv 1812.06600](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06600)
- Nagy, Calliess, Zohren (Oxford-Man, Frontiers in AI 2023): APEX deep double dueling Q-learning with LSTM in an ABIDES-based gym on AAPL LOBSTER data (train Jan-May 2012, test May-Jun 2012, robustness on 2022 data), executing a 10-s directional signal with inventory ±10 shares; RL beats a heuristic passive/aggressive baseline by **+20.7 pp (low noise), +14.8 pp (mid), +32.2 pp (high noise)** with significant Sharpe gains. Limitations: only temporary direct impact ("cannot account for the indirect or permanent component"), no counterparty reaction or adverse selection modeling, single stock, tiny inventory. — [Frontiers in AI](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1151003/full)
- Modular RL execution framework — [arXiv 2208.06244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06244); multi-agent simulator execution — [arXiv 2411.06389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06389); LOB simulation review — [arXiv 2402.17359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17359); RL-in-finance survey — [arXiv 2411.12746](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.12746)

**Production claims**
- JPMorgan LOXM: deployed in Europe early 2017, global by year-end; trained on "billions of past trades, both real and simulated"; a survey of equity traders indicated ~15% improvement in execution efficiency (secondary sources; no TCA methodology published). — [Medium (Mosic)](https://ranko-mosic.medium.com/reinforcement-learning-based-trading-application-at-jp-morgan-chase-f829b8ec54f2); [DigitalDefynd case study](https://digitaldefynd.com/IQ/jp-morgan-using-ai-case-study/); [Informa Connect on LOXM](https://informaconnect.com/the-latest-in-loxm-and-why-we-shouldnt-be-using-single-stock-algos/) — all secondary; no primary JPM document with numbers found.
- Man AHL: uses RL for execution routing ("best route to market"), not security selection; adaptive intelligent routing; no public performance numbers. — [Man Group insights](https://www.man.com/insights/the-big-picture-ai); [Man Institute ML page](https://www.man.com/maninstitute/machine-learning)

**Simulator bias evidence relevant to all RL results**
- Same execution algorithms ranked differently under replay vs queue-reactive vs zero-intelligence simulators at 50 ms latency: replay: CKadv < AC < TWAP < VWAP < CK; queue-reactive: TWAP < VWAP < CK < CKadv < AC; a markout-aware OFI scheduler beat TWAP by 4.2 bp in replay but *lost* 1.7 bp in queue-reactive mode. — [lob-execution-sim](https://github.com/m-ichaels/lob-execution-sim)

### Inferences
- Model choice for a low-latency engine: gradient-boosted trees or logistic/linear models on engineered OFI/imbalance/trade-flow features are the evidence-backed default (equal or better accuracy than DeepLOB on Bybit; Kolm et al. show engineered stationary flow inputs matter more than architecture; inference latency is bounded). Deep sequence models are justified only if a measured accuracy gain survives fees and the 100 ms-scale inference budget.
- Reported RL gains of 2-23 bps over TWAP on crypto should be discounted heavily: they are single-asset, one-month OOS, on replay-with-parametric-impact simulators, and the sign of the gain can flip under a different simulator. Treat RL execution as academic-only for crypto; the two production deployments (JPM, Man AHL) are equities/futures/FX and publish no verifiable numbers.
- Known failure modes to design against: (1) replay simulators over-fill passive orders ~2x and ignore counterparty reaction; (2) crypto models decay within days-weeks, so retraining plus shadow-gated deployment is required; (3) VWAP/TWAP benchmarks built from displayed depth flatter learned policies; (4) accuracy metrics overstate actionability (Briola et al.); (5) agents fail to learn "do nothing" when signals are noisy (Nagy et al.).

### Gaps
- Nevmyvaka et al.'s exact improvement figures (commonly quoted as >50% vs submit-and-leave) were not verifiable from the retrievable PDF text; treat as unconfirmed.
- Schnaubelt's bps improvement over TWAP is not stated in the abstract; the "~2 bps" figure is from an unverified search summary.
- No primary-source performance numbers for LOXM, Man AHL, Two Sigma, Bloomberg execution ML; no crypto-native firm publishes execution-ML results.

## Key Question 5: Practitioner consensus — what do execution teams actually deploy?

### Takeaway
Publicly documented practice is simple and rules/regression based: Quantitative Brokers states "no part of the system is machine learning" for the core execution engine (10-15 microstructure inputs per market, regime-adaptive weights calibrated offline, human evaluation), using ML only for offline pattern discovery; crypto market-making reference implementations use standardized depth imbalance to skew quotes; RL is publicly confirmed in production only at JPM (LOXM) and Man AHL, with no verifiable TCA numbers. Crypto MMs (Wintermute, Jump, HRT) publish no execution-signal specifics.

### Cited Findings
- **Quantitative Brokers (Almgren):** "ten or fifteen inputs per market" (average quote size, spread, realized vol, realized volume, clustered into efficient/normal/challenging regimes); "the weighting formula adapts with regimes"; "It is important to stress that no part of the system is machine learning. Parameters are calibrated offline with common sense"; VAE and RL used only for offline pattern discovery and multi-algo portfolio optimization; "Everything is evaluated by humans, and nothing is fully automated"; no bps TCA numbers disclosed. — [The Hedge Fund Journal on QB](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/qb-a-new-era-in-quantitative-execution-microstructure-regimes/); QB research listing incl. cross-impact and DRL notes — [QB whitepapers](https://www.quantitativebrokers.com/whitepapers)
- Signal-adaptive optimal trading theory that practitioners cite (Almgren-type with signals): [Lehalle & Neuman, Incorporating Signals into Optimal Trading, arXiv 1704.00847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.00847); [Optimal Signal-Adaptive Trading with Temporary and Transient Impact, arXiv 2002.09549](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.09549)
- "Order flow feedback is anecdotally incorporated in most implemented practitioner algorithms"; market makers shift placement deeper in response to one-sided market-order flow. — [Optimal Execution with Dynamic OFI, arXiv 1409.2618](https://arxiv.org/html/1409.2618v2)
- Crypto MM reference implementation (hftbacktest, Binance Futures with real latency data): standardized multi-level imbalance -> fair price skew -> reservation price with inventory skew; per-trade edge fell from 0.0139% (May 2023) to 0.0086% (Feb 2025). — [hftbacktest tutorial](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/Market%20Making%20with%20Alpha%20-%20Order%20Book%20Imbalance.html)
- Vendor/educational crypto MM guidance describes the same pattern: dynamic quote adjustment from order-book imbalance with low-latency data. — [AllTick blog](https://alltick.co/blog/crypto-market-making-strategy-dynamically-adjusting-quotes-using-order-book-imbalance)
- JPM LOXM (RL, equities, in production since 2017; ~15% efficiency per trader survey, secondary sources) and Man AHL (RL for execution routing) — see KQ4 citations. [LIVE, no primary numbers]
- Wintermute: quant strategies "analyze order book depth, historical volatility patterns, and cross-exchange arbitrage" and integrate with CoinRoutes for algorithmic execution — no signal specifics. — [Wintermute press](https://www.wintermute.com/wintermute-integrates-with-coinroutes-a-leading-provider-of-algorithmic-trading-solutions); [Bitget overview](https://www.bitget.com/amp/academy/wintermute-crypto). Jump: "tens of thousands of trades per day ... margins often measured in basis points or less" — [Insights4VC](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/inside-jump-crypto-13b-terra-trade). HRT publishes only blockchain-data research tooling — [HRT blog](https://www.hudsonrivertrading.com/hrtbeat/enabling-algo-research-blockchain/).
- Peer-reviewed crypto evidence agrees with the simple-model stance: tree models/logistic regression match DeepLOB on Bybit ([arXiv 2506.05764](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05764v2)); gradient boosting outperforms deep nets in crypto price prediction comparisons ([Springer Discover AI 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-025-00519-y)).

### Inferences
- The deployable stack that the evidence supports: (1) a regime classifier on spread/vol/volume/depth (QB-style, 10-15 inputs); (2) OFI + multi-level imbalance + trade-flow for micro-timing and quote skew; (3) a fill-probability model from near/opposite queue sizes plus an adverse-selection markout model for post-vs-cross; (4) square-root impact for child sizing; (5) an adaptive schedule (Almgren-type with signals) rather than a learned end-to-end policy. RL, if used at all, belongs in offline parameter search / schedule tuning, mirroring how QB and Man AHL describe their use.

### Gaps
- No crypto market maker or prop firm (Wintermute, Jump, HRT, Jane Street, XTX) publishes execution-signal or model details; no Talos/Anboto/Coinbase/Kraken execution-quality studies with bps numbers were found.
- No public TCA numbers from any production RL execution system.

## Key Question 6: Backtesting execution algorithms correctly — simulators, data, latency figures

### Takeaway
Correct execution backtests need (i) L2 (preferably L3) tick data with exchange and local timestamps, (ii) explicit feed and order latency models calibrated from probe orders, (iii) a queue-position model validated against real fills, and (iv) an impact/resilience model, because replay alone doubles short-horizon fill rates, algorithm rankings flip across simulator types, and live Bybit/Binance probes show latency-driven fill worsening and fail-to-fill for marketable limits. Typical figures: ~5 ms exchange-side processing at Binance, 20-25 ms RTT from AWS Tokyo vs ~270 ms from Europe, and 90-180 ms feed delays observed from non-colocated recorders.

### Cited Findings
- **hftbacktest (nkaz001):** Python/Rust backtester for HFT/MM that models feed latency and order latency and queue position, using full tick data for trades and L2/L3 books, with Binance/Bybit examples. Order latency models: ConstantLatency, BackwardFeedLatency, ForwardFeedLatency, FeedLatency (average of latest/next feed latencies), and IntpOrderLatency ("the most accurate ... if you have the data with a fine time interval"), which interpolates from measured (local request time, exchange timestamp, local response time) triples; the docs recommend collecting latency data "by submitting unexecutable orders regularly". Queue models: RiskAverse, PowerProb2/3, SquareProb, LogProb2. — [hftbacktest latency models](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/v1.8.4/latency_models.html); [queue models tutorial](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/Probability%20Queue%20Models.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/nkaz001/hftbacktest)
- **Queue-model validation and replay bias (Bitstamp L3, 21,687 real touch orders; Binance/Coinbase L2):** L2 queue models overestimate queue-ahead by 21-29%; replay fills 0.17/0.58/0.75 at 5/30/120 s vs real 0.07/0.30/0.62; algorithm cost rankings differ across replay / queue-reactive / zero-intelligence modes; measured feed delay (receive minus exchange timestamp) p50/p95: **Binance 169/182 ms, Coinbase 105/140 ms, Bitstamp 89 ms** (non-colocated recorder). — [lob-execution-sim](https://github.com/m-ichaels/lob-execution-sim)
- **Live probe evidence for latency modelling (Bybit 3M+ orders in one week, Binance 40k market orders):** discrepancies between expected (last snapshot) and realized fills are driven by exchange delays, trader-side delays and network delays; market orders fill worse than expected; marketable limits have substantial fail-to-fill-immediately probability; effects scale with volatility, latency and liquidity; paper supplies parsimonious P(fail-to-fill) and price-worsening models to close the backtest-vs-live gap. — [Albers et al., Quantitative Finance 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14697688.2025.2515933) [LIVE]
- **Exchange-side latency figures:** Binance Futures says its platform processes up to 100,000 orders/s with average latency of 5 ms. — [Binance blog](https://www.binance.com/en/blog/futures/binance-futures-for-institutions--pioneering-performance--technology-421499824684900642); Binance offers a Futures low-latency API service — [Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/detail/7df7f3838c3b49e692d175374c3a3283). Matching engine in AWS Tokyo (ap-northeast-1): ~20-25 ms RTT from Tokyo, ~270 ms from Europe. — [Arbitron latency map](https://arbitron.app/learn/crypto-exchange-server-locations) (vendor page). One practitioner article reports a median order round-trip of 884 ms from AWS Tokyo with ~879 ms server-side (likely under load / REST) — [Medium (Keller)](https://medium.com/@laostjen/high-frequency-trading-in-crypto-latency-infrastructure-and-reality-594e994132fd) (single unverified measurement; conflicts with Binance's 5 ms claim; both should be checked with your own probe orders).
- **ABIDES-based RL environments** reconstruct LOB from message data and model only temporary direct impact by injecting orders into historical flow — permanent impact and counterparty reactions are absent. — [Nagy et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1151003/full)
- **Queue-reactive simulators** (Huang-Lehalle-Rosenbaum) and deep-learning-QR simulators provide state-dependent flow and are used as an alternative to replay. — [arXiv 1312.0563](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563); [arXiv 2501.08822](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.08822); review of LOB simulation approaches — [arXiv 2402.17359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17359)
- **Impact in replay:** RL-Exec adds endogenous transient impact with exponential resilience, partial fills, fees and latency on top of replay. — [arXiv 2511.07434](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07434v1)
- Crypto model decay implies backtests must be walk-forward with frequent retraining and gated deployment. — [arXiv 2607.28577](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28577)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable simulator for this engine: hftbacktest-style event-driven replay with (a) IntpOrderLatency calibrated from your own unexecutable probe orders per venue, (b) feed latency from exchange-vs-local timestamps, (c) a queue model chosen by matching backtest fill curves to a small live sample (the Binance 0.36/0.67/0.84 at 5/30/120 s curve is a first reference), (d) a fail-to-fill and price-worsening model for marketable orders (Albers et al.), and (e) square-root transient impact with resilience for aggressive children. Validate any algorithm ranking across at least two simulator modes before trusting it.
- Latency budget to model: exchange processing single-digit ms (Binance claim), network 20-25 ms if in Tokyo AWS, 100-300 ms if not; feed delays of 90-180 ms were observed by non-colocated recorders, which is longer than the 100 ms horizon at which imbalance signals have most of their power, so signal value must be evaluated at (horizon + realized round-trip), not at the nominal horizon.

### Gaps
- No authoritative published distribution (percentiles) of Binance/Bybit order acknowledgement or fill latency from a colocated setup; figures above are vendor claims or single-user measurements. Nautilus Trader latency/queue modelling documentation was not reviewed (no source retrieved).
- Full text of the Albers et al. latency paper (with percentiles and bps worsening) was not accessible.
